ABSTRACT

There are more than fifty species of spider mites have been recorded in
Taiwan. The following species are considered the major pests on their host
plants, i. e. citrus red spider mite, Panonychus citri (McGregor), citrus rust
mite, Phyllocoptruta oleivora Ashmead on citrus; two-spotted spider mite,
Tetranychus urticae (Koch) on deciduous fruit trees and strawberry; european
red spider mite, P. zln1i (Koch) on deciduous fruit trees; kanzawa spider mite,
T. kanzawai Kishida on tea, strawberry and mulberry. Thirty species of native
predators were recorded, including 14 species predatory insects and 16 species
of predatory mites. Stethorus loi Sasaji, Oligota oviformis (Casey), Arthrocnodax
occidentalis Felt., Amblyseius longispinosus (Evans), A. womersleyi Schicha, A.
ovalis (Evans), and A. herbicolus (Chant) were identified as more important
ones,

The infestation of citrus red mite is becoming rather severe in citrus
orchards since its beneficial predators are sacrified by the extensive and
frequent use of insecticides. A serious damage to citrus fruit caused by citrus
rust mite. Recently, it was found commonly infested by an entomogenous
fungus, Hirsutella thompsonit Fish. The fungus’ mycelia were used for field
spray in order to evaluate its pathogenicity and effectiveness in the rust - mite
control. Encouraged results were obtained.

Five species of Amblyseius mites associated with kanzawa spider mite in
the mulberry groves. Field distribution pattern of A. womersleyi did match

kanzawa spider mite population distribution. A. womersleyi was evaluated as

the best biocontrol agent for kanzawa spider mite population. Mass releases of
this predators to the mulberry groves, bringing about satisfactory control.

Kanzawa spider mite and two spotted spider mite also attack strawberry
in Taiwan. Mass releases of A. longispinosus resulted in an: overall 85%
reduction in the kanzawa spider. mite population after 4 weeks initial releases.
But for two-spotted spider mits, it gave poor control results. Recently, a new
phytoseiid, A. californicus (McGregor) has been introduced from Hawaii, this
predatory mite is relatively easy to mass-rear. Plots in which predators were
primarily mass released had established and lower populations of JT. urticae.
Further field colonization is now in progress.

INTRODUCTION

Before the ‘DDT era’ mite pests and

their natural enemies on crops had

received attention from only a few people.
However, shortly after DDT and other
synthetic organic insecticides had become
widely used, reports of outbreaks of
tetranychid mites on various crops began
to appear frequently in entomological
literature. These outbreaks of mites in
the early 1960’s to 1970’s created an
impetus for studying plant-feeding mites
and their predators. Research since that
time can be classified into five different
phases, which have overlapped chronologi-
cally: 1). Faunistic surveys, 2). Biological
and ecological. studies, 3). Field and
insectary studies, 4). Implementation of
biological. control of mites on crops, and
5). Introduction and establishment of
phytoseiids. Classical biological control of
mites in Taiwan has not been conducted
until quite recently, only about five years
ago. The present paper jis concerned with

the biological -control of mite pests on
citrus, deciduous fruit :trees, mulberry,
tea, and strawberry.

Population . density of Amblyseius ovalis

of the spider mite. Seasonal population

the population densities and distribution

synchrony with those of the spider mites
and hence were not effective biological
control agents. A. womersleyi was eva-
luated to be the best biocontrol agent
for spider mite control among the’ five
tested predators.

The cost of mass-rearing the
predatory mite, A. womersleyi was
estimated on the basis of producing 20
and 200 million predators per year. The
cost of maintenance and quality control
of the stock colony for the mass rearing
of predators and their prey was added
into the cost of mass-rearing ‘the
predators. The estimated cost was 0.0085
and 0.006 CNT$) per predator when 20
and 200 million predators, respectively,
were reared. Mass releases of this
predator at the rate of 100,000 predators
per hectare in the mulberry groves are
adequate to give-satisfactory mite control.

CONCLUSIONS

Although more than 50 species of
phytoseiids and 20 species of insect
predators of mite pests have been recor-
ded in Taiwan,.only a few are considered
to be of value. Generally these few
species are abundant on commercial
crops. There have been few investigations
on the complex of phytoseiid mites and
insect predators to "determine whether
two or more species are more effective
than one alone. However, in the meantime
introduction of predators from other areas
should. be. continued.

_ Finally, to increase the utilization of
phytoseiids in- biological control and pest
management programs, additional staff in
research, extension, and mass production
is required. Educational and. extension
programs are just as important as research.
The key to success is the devlopment of
a crop protection system, which involves
monitoring of populations, application
technical knowledge, and critical decision
making at the right ‘time. Biological
control programs against of mite pests
in the future may be more economical,
more effective, and safer than complete

reliance on- chemical control.
