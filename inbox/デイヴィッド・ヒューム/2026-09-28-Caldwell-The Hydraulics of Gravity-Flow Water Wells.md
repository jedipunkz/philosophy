THE HYDRAULICS OF GRAVITY-FLOW
WATER WELLS

The purpose of this thesis was to make a thorough mathe-
matical analysis of the hydraulics of gravity-flow water wells.
During the investigation it was found that rigid mathematical
analysis of some aspects of the problem was impossible and in
those cases the results of tests on actual flow models were
used to obtain semi-empirical solutions.

The literature relating to the movement of underground
water especially applicable to gravity-flow phenomena has
been reviewed and a bibliography with short abstracts of each
article is presented.

The significance of the coefficient of permeability in Dar-
cy’s law is discussed and the various methods for the measure-
ment thereof have been classified as laboratory, indirect and
field methods. Each method of measurement is reviewed
briefly.

The range of validity of Darcy’s law is discussed and it is
concluded from the results of the most precise tests available
that there is no “minimum hydraulic gradient.” The upper
limiting velocity where Darcy’s law may be expected to fail is
given by a Reynolds number of 1.

The general theory of the partial penetration of a water-
bearing stratum by a single gravity well and a theory of in-
terference effects in small groups of wells have been adapted
from Muskat and presented in brief form. Tests of the inter-
ference effects of small groups of wells have been compared
with the theoretical equations and, as a result, it has been
concluded that the theoretical equations are valid.
