Prayers to begin at Eleven o Clock.

HE Parents of the Sunday School Children are allowed, by one branch of this

Charity, to purchaſe Clothing for ſuch Children as regularly attend the Sunday
Schools, with an Allowance of one quarter in the price; by which Means they are
enabled to pay for the Schooling of one or two Children at the Day School, and for
no greater Expence than they muſt otherwiſe be at merely for their Clothing :* The
Children alſo making their own things in the Schools of Induſtry, ſtill reduces the
Price, whilſt, at the ſame time they are furniſhed with the Materials for Inſtruction.

By this Plan the Parents have certainly been taught the habit of Economy, and
the value of Induſtry, by being led to make the beſt uſe of every little Sum of Money,
which however ſmall, by that means they really feel the benefit of. Whereas, with-
out ſuch an Opportunity of turning their ſmall Savings to Account, they probably
would have appeared too trifling to go to market with; and nothing can more ſtrongly
reſtify the great utility of this part of the Plan, than the evident improvement every
Year in the appearance of the Children, who with this ſmall Aſſiſtance are thoroughly
well clothed by the Induſtry of their Parents; for the Benefactors are aſſured, that it
is one particular Rule of the Charity not to furniſh any Clothing gratis, (except for
occafional Rewards to ſome of the Scholars: ) Experience having proved that giving
a Spur to Induſtry is a real kindneſs, whilſt Donations, too often, act as a Temp-
tation to Idleneſs.

The Attention of the Benefactors is drawn to this circumſtance, as the original
Tagged appearance of the Children is certainly in the recollection of moſt of thoſe who
have ſo kindly perſevered in their annual Contributions. It can not therefore but be
a gratifying reward to their liberality, to ſee ſo large a number of children ſupplied
by their means with the comfort of cheap and decent Clothing, as well as Inſtruction,
and to know that many of them have already been enabled to get their own Liveli-
hood, and taught to do their Duty in Life. Theſe pleaſing reflections cannot fail to

open the ſame ſources of Benevolence to ſupport the continuation of the Plan for
