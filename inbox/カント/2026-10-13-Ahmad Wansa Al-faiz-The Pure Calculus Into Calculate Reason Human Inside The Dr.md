Abstract.

This paper explores the conceptual framework of "Pure Calculus" as a
fundamental operating system of human cognition that transcends conventional
mathematical computation. Through the lens of Freudian dream interpretation, we
examine how human reasoning functions as a form of embodied calculation that
transforms material forms into abstract concepts via unconscious computational
processes. The research proposes that dream imagery, exemplified by impossible
combinations such as "pink cows," represents traces of cognitive processes that can be
analyzed as "animation potentials" revealing the pathways of imagination and embodied
cognition.

Introduction.

The evolution of human theoretical intelligence finds its most elementary
foundation in the concept of naming (konsep nama), where the underlying basis for
nomenclature and notational tendencies derives from material forms. This fundamental
principle suggests that abstract theoretical content emerges through what Freud
describes as the accumulation of phenomenal experience in dream interpretation,
where reality factors transform into abstract dogma through systematic unconscious
processing.

The term "Pure Calculus" designates not mathematical computation in
its formal sense, but rather the fundamental computational system underlying human
cognition—a process by which the mind performs operations that transform material
reality into symbolic structures. This calculus operates through correspondences rather
than conventional numerical relationships, engaging what we term "embodied
mathematics" where abstract concepts find their origins in sensory and corporeal
experience.

The Genealogy of Theoretical Intelligence.

Human cognitive development follows a discernible progression from
material forms through naming conventions to abstract conceptual frameworks. This
evolutionary pathway can be mapped as: Material Form » Name » Notation » Abstract
Concept » Theoretical Dogma. Each stage represents a computational transformation
where embodied experience undergoes systematic abstraction while maintaining traces
of its material origins.

The naming process itself reveals the dependence of abstract thought
upon material foundations. When we examine linguistic arbitrariness—such as the
absence of the letter "M" in the physical object we call "meja" (table)—we discover that
the relationship between signifier and signified, while arbitrary in Saussurean terms,
nonetheless emerges from embodied interactions with material reality. The "pure
calculus" operates precisely in this liminal space where material experience becomes
symbolic representation.

Dream-Work as Computational Process.
Within the Freudian framework of dream interpretation, the unconscious

performs sophisticated computational operations that transform phenomenal
experiences into symbolic narratives. The dream represents an "area domain" where

pure calculus manifests most clearly, unencumbered by the constraints of logical
consistency or empirical verification that govern waking consciousness.

The example of "sapi berwarna pink" (pink cow) illustrates what we term
"animation potential"—the capacity of imagination to recombine existing material
elements (the concept of "cow" # the concept of "pink color") into configurations
impossible within empirical reality yet meaningful within the computational logic of
dream-work. This process reveals that even the most surreal imaginative combinations
remain constrained by "factors of existence that already exist as reality," as Freud
demonstrates in his analysis of dream condensation and displacement mechanisms.

Embodied Cognition and Dominant Systems.

Recent developments in understanding dream causality suggest that
dream imagery may be influenced by dominant embodied systems within the body or
specific brain areas that function as control systems for information processing and
regulation of bodily and emotional mechanisms. This embodied dimension adds a
psychosomatic layerto the pure calculus framework, suggesting that the computational
processes underlying dream-work are not purely symbolic but are materially grounded
in physiological states and neural activities.

The dominant embodied system hypothesis proposes that specific types
of dream imagery correspond to the activation of particular bodily systems: digestive
system dominance may produce food-related imagery, motor cortex activation may
generate movement or spatial imagery, and emotional regulation system stress may
result in surreal combinations that reflect attempts at psychic eguilibrium. This suggests
that "pure calculus" operates through embodied computational processes that integrate
cognitive, emotional, and somatic information.

Deconstructive Methodology and Narrative Critigue.

The application of deconstructive methodology within this framework
involves what can be understood as "membongkar" (careful dismantling) rather than
destructive analysis. Human civilization, accepted as positive value in essence,
represents a systematic "dis" (destruction) of components within the composition of the
universe—exemplified by breaking stones for house foundations, where the act of
breaking constitutes destruction of structure to achieve architectural aesthetics.

This deconstructive process operates within dream interpretation
through "kritik narasi" (narrative critigue), a method for carefully dismantling inadeguate
narrative structures and reconstructing more suitable ones without completely
destroying foundational beliefs. The pure calculus thus functions as both constructive

Implications for Cognitive Science and Hermeneutics.

The Pure Calculus framework offers significant implications for
understanding the relationship between embodied cognition and symbolic thought. By
positioning dream interpretation as a computational domain where material experience
undergoes systematic transformation, we can better understand how human reasoning
operates as a form of embodied calculation that transcends the boundaries between
rational thought and unconscious processing.

This approach also provides methodological insights for textual
interpretation and creative practice. The capacity to extract meaning from minimal
fragments—whether through "distant reading" methodologies or fragmentary textual
encounters—can be understood as applications of pure calculus principles, where
partial information undergoes computational processing to generate comprehensive
interpretive frameworks.

The framework suggests that dream interpretation functions as a
