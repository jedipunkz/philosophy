## Abstract

This paper examines the concept of the person, rather than the individual or institution, as the foundational unit for understanding philanthropic values such as voluntarism and relationality. When philanthropy is framed through individual or institutional lenses, assistance can be interpreted as a loss of agency for either giver or receiver. In contrast, Christian personalism offers a more wholistic philosophical basis for philanthropic action by arguing that what is most deeply one’s own is also oriented toward others. The paper outlines personalism’s critique of individual- and institution-centered models of social change and its implications for voluntary action for the public good. The paper then considers Søren Kierkegaard’s strategic use of a variety of direct and indirect writing styles as a way of translating Personalism’s critique into an effective cure for philanthropy itself. This treatment is capable of reorienting the foundations of philanthropy, thus enabling a more free and ethical style of help that is attuned toward a good common to both the helped and the helpers.
