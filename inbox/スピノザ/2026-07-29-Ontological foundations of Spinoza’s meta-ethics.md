## Abstract

The article provides a comparative analysis of ethical postulates by Spinoza, an outstanding representative of modern European philosophy, and modern meta-ethics theorists' views on the nature of morality. The search for ontological foundations of morality, carried out by Spinoza in Ethics and based on the concepts of “substance” (Latin substantia), “God” (Latin Deus) and “nature” (Latin natura), correlates with discussions about the nature of morality in modern analytical philosophy and with attempts to logically substantiate true ideas about the Good. The article examines beliefs of meta-ethics representatives in various areas in comprehending both fundamental and specific ethical issues aimed at recreating the true picture of being through a special logical analysis of language and identifying the true rules of thinking. The comparative analysis of terminology allows us to reveal the concept of meta-ethics as a theory of morality that includes in its problem field not only a special language, but also metaphysics and epistemology, as well as to signify the range of modern meta-ethical theories of naturalism, non-naturalism and theological voluntarism. The author draws on the works of domestic and foreign specialists on Spinoza's philosophy - A. G. Gadzhikurbanov, J. Deleuze, K. Fischer, as well as articles and books on meta-ethics by Western authors - A. Fisher, R. Shafer-Landau, D. Copp. The purpose of the article is to reveal the means by which the ontological basis of Spinoza's teaching influences the ethical side of the issue, which makes it possible to establish a connection between the ethical doctrine of the Dutch thinker with twentieth-century meta-ethical philosophers' views. Interrelation of Spinoza's “naturalistic” position in understanding ethics with the ideas of analytical philosophy is revealed. Reasons are clarified why Spinoza's views can be considered to be propositions of theology, non-naturalism and naturalism. The article provides a new perspective on the relationship between ontology and ethics in Spinoza's teaching.
