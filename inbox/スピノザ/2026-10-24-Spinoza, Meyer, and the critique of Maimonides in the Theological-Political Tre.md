## Abstract

ABSTRACT This paper defends a still-contested view: that Baruch Spinoza’s critique of Maimonides in Chapter 7 of the Theological-Political Treatise was a response to Lodewijk Meyer’s Philosophia S. Scripturae interpres. It substantiates this claim with two new arguments. First, it shows that Spinoza’s presentation of Maimonides’s position departs from Maimonides’s own words and instead closely corresponds to a passage in Chapter 14 of Meyer’s work. Second, it argues that much of Spinoza’s critique makes sense only when read as directed against Meyer. On this basis, the paper concludes that Spinoza closely studied Meyer’s work, borrowed its formulations, and sought to expose the consequences of Meyer’s position. It thus challenges the common assumption that Spinoza was intellectually superior to Meyer and shows instead that, at least in matters of biblical hermeneutics, Spinoza regarded Meyer’s arguments as requiring careful engagement and even drew on Meyer’s own formulations in order to criticize them. Finally, the paper suggests that, in light of this conclusion and of Meyer’s extensive knowledge of Reformed theology, Spinoza likely relied on Meyer as well when, under the name of Alfakhar, he set out the Reformed method of biblical interpretation.
