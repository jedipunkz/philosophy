## Abstract

This investigation is presented as an exegetic exercise of Baruj Spinoza’s philosophy of immanence that tries to underline how the human freedom is possible thru the affective dynamic. To do so, it offers a comprehensive reading of the Ethics demonstrated in geometrical order based on two fronts: the theory of kinds of knowledge and the metaphysic foundations of Spinoza’s philosophy; aspects in which the perspective of the human nature and its concept conatus, new for his time but actual to ours, is braked down. The first part reviews the critique to the prejudice in which the philosopher of geometric writing takes distance from cartesianism and common sense negating the notion of freewill. The second part examines the strategy propose to moderate the affects as a point of entrance to the ethics, this last one understood as construction of common notion of the affectivity or as an adequate knowledge of affects. The third and last part questions the possibility and the consequences of understanding the human affects in relation with the intuitive science and the intellectual love to god as a way of power of the intellect (freedom).
