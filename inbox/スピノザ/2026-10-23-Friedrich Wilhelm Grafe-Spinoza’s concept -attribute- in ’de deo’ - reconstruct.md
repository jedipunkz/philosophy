Abstract

This reconstructional working paper on the concept "attribute" in
the first book ["de deo"] of Spinoza’s "ethics" expands the use of the
three coordinative definitions, specifying model theoretic analoga for "at-
tribute", "mode", and "substance" - which have been introduced in
Grafe [8]. Main leverage for this reconstructional project "attribute" is,
to take Spinoza’s definition [e1d4]1 literally. Basically in this paper two
questions will be considered, starting from axiom 1 of "de deo" [for short:
e1a1], viz., "omnia, quae sunt, vel in se, vel in alio sunt", where
Spinoza seems to partition the class of all things in two disjunct classes,
the class of things, which are in themselves, and the class of things, which
are in other things. This prompts

question (1)
Is this partitioning view (two disjunct non-empty classes of
things) compatible with the monist view, that there exists noth-
ing besides (the one) substance in its affections, and if compat-
ible, how ?
The partitioning view is reflected explicitly in George Boole’s Spinoza
study (-> [1] chapter XIII §§16-18, cf.
§17).
And a reconstructional
answer available shows question (1) resolved for the modeltheoretic cor-
relates of "substance" and "mode" specified in [8]2.

question (2)
If by e1a1 nothing exists besides substance(viz., in it’s affec-
tions), then what’s the ontological status of attributes ?
In my (again reconstructional) answer to question (2) I argue, that,
in any tenable interpretation of e1d4, Spinoza’s concept of "attribute"
need be explicated as intrinsically relational.

1the label e1d4 e.g., following a meanwhile in Spinoza scholarship widely shared referencing
convention - will be used to refer to definition 4 of part 1 "de deo" of Baruch (Benedict)
de Spinoza’s "Ethics" (Latin text cited from [13] , Gebhardt pagination in [ ... ], English
translation, where explicitly cited, is Curley’s [14], free English paraphrasing (often) my own).

2with the upshot, that both - the partitioning view and the monist stance - are compatible
and hold for the modeltheoretic correlates. This compatability implies a fading away of any
supposed ontological relevance of the partitioning view. And this again helps understand the
monist view’s rational reconstruction enterprise.

This study of Spinoza’s concept "attribute" in e1 aims at sharpening, deepen-

of [8], focussing on the ontological status of attributes, with a long-term ob-

orthogonal to rather all current discussion of "attribute" in e1, which has

1.2
Spinoza’s basic "de deo" intention or working plan

which imop presumably led to this great architecture of monist metaphysics,
and this then is my respective perception:
(1) Spinoza sees as the challenge for metaphysical studies in his time the
mission, to reconcile (1a) the best of ancient substance metaphysics with the
at his time current developments in (1b) the physical science (=philosophy of
nature)4. And Spinoza is fully aware, that the respective metaphysical approach
by Descartes fails to achieve such reconciliation; regarding perhaps as their
greatest ’sin’ the doctrine of ’created substances’ [which might fairly be judged
a ’contradictio in adiecto’ 5], and wrt Descartes stress on (non vacuous) metric
space Spinoza takes measures by changing the emphasis of extensio to motion
and rest6, implicitly thence suggesting this metric space being only a derivative
from these.
(2) And Spinoza’s response to Descartes’ poor workmanship wrt (1a) is, to
develop an intransigently rigid concept of substance7, characterized only very
general by some "propria of substance". But (wrt 1b) Spinoza ropes up this
metaphysical concept owed to (1a) by his definition of deus8 for the
integration of the at his time current science with an in this peculiarity
totally new concept of "attribute of substance", using "attribute" as
the placeholder for the new physics [stating metrical laws and suggesting their
experimental, else observational, verification], which in the 17th century is the
upcoming perception of nature [=of substance in its affections]9.

3
reconstructional approach

3.1
basics and motivation

Grafe [8] stipulates three coordinative definitions for the e1 concepts "sub-
stance", "mode", "attribute", in other words, starts a partial 1-1 mapping

{ concepts of e1 } ↦ { concepts of modeltheory }15

The idea of this partial mapping, and - in fact - its validation as a tool for
e1 rational reconstruction is, to show significant similarity of interrelations of
central concepts in "de deo" with the interelations of the coordinated concepts
in standard model theory, i.e., the mapping of the "de deo" concepts is created
and expected to provide a non trivial homomorphism from concepts "de deo"
to concepts of model theory. And a key point to achieve this had been found
and developed with the mapping for "mode" based on the perhaps somewhat
unexpected but imop not only convincing but even forced, mapping for the
"de deo" concept "attribute" to the model theoretic concept theory of a
relational structure
The more general idea then is to find thence prospects and/or guidance for
a systematic elaboration of an enduring rationale of Spinoza’s e1 = "de deo"

15"modeltheory" in this paper always (unless explicitly noted otherwise) refers as usual to
the set theoretic interpretation of standard systems of first order logic

7

metaphysics by developing and considering corresponding constructs in model
theory and reframing (some of the) most difficult interpretation questions of
"de deo" as questions wrt the logical interrelations of the corresponding model
theoretic constructs; in the hope, that significant answers can be found there
and can - by inverse mapping - be applied to the original, viz., to "de deo".

3.2
formalism

The short form of the formalism used for the coordinative definitions, is:

consider an attribute - e.g."extension" - given as pair, consisting of a (large
scale) theory Th (formalized in first order logic), and its intended model
< U,Rα >, then
for each theorem Θ of theory Th wrt the intended model of Th, < U,Rα >,
1. select a prenex normal form Γ of Θ, viz., pnf(Θ) = Γ = QPΓ ∩MΓ, where
QPΓ is the quantifier Prefix, and MΓ the quantifier free Matrix of the selected
prenex normal form Γ of Θ.
2. now, for each theorem Θ of Th :
the model theoretic object representing a mode perceived by this attribute
is the value of its correlated pnf-Matrix MΓ, viz., VMΓ, in the model < U,Rα >
3. the model theoretic object representing the substance perceived by this
attribute then is the union of the sets VMΓ of the pnf ′s matrices MΓ of all
theorems Θ of theory Th.
For motivation, background and more technical detail of this construct see [8]
and [7], for a mini-example for a mode from celestial mechanics see [9], section
6.216.

3.3
overview and comment

The e1 concept "attribute" (e1d4) maps to (i.e., has as it’s model theoretic
corrrelate) the concept "theory (first order) of a relational structure" in
the model theoretic standard sense, ([8], §4.1, p.12).
This coordinative definition and a to this end newly elaborated semantic
consequence of the first order logic "prenex normal form"- theorem
allow create a seminal coordinative definition for the e1 concept "mode", as
perceived by an "attribute"([8] §4.2, p.12f.
), and thence derivatively for
a coordinative definition of the e1 concept "substance", as perceived by an
"attribute" ([8], §4.3„ p.14f)
An important aspect to keep in mind were, that - in this reconstructional
scenario - for any attribute the model theoretic picture for each mode is
determined uniquely up to logical equivalence by the model theoretic picture
of the attribute, which may be thought of as picturing a clear and distinct
perception of a mode by an attribute. And as a consequence, the unique
determination of the mode-pictures implies the unique determination of the
substance-picture. Taking the inverse mapping gives us the not unexpected but
plausible unique determination of substance, as perceived by an attribute
by this attribute.

4.2
answer & explanation

In order to answer the question, I transpose Boole’s hitherto "disjunctions" re-
sult to the model theoretic reconstructional setting, where an answer is easily
available. The only formal means needed for this transposition is use of the
powerset algebra of a given set, i.e., the meanwhile commonplace use of set the-
oretic inclusion, intersection and union for a given countable (finite or countably
infinite) set17.
Now, agreeing with Boole’s already mentioned observation of the equivalence
of Spinoza’s "disjunctions", in order to answer question (1) it’s sufficient to
consider the parallel between the first and the third disjunction:

As already noted above in the paragraph on formalism:
for a given model theoretic picture of an attribute the model theoretic picture
of substance, as perceived by this attribute is defined as the set theoretical
union of the model theoretic pictures of all modes, as perceived by this
attribute.

Accordingly in the model theoretic picture we have both:
First, as in the model theoretic picture, substance is a set, the power set of
substance exists, and as well substance itself, and all its modes are mutually
different elements of this powerset.
Secondly, this great multiplicity has but no ontological import, because the
model theoretic picture of substance contains the model theoretical pictures
of all modes, though not as elements, but as subsets.

Summing up, the answer to question (1) in the model theoretic pic-
ture is to the positive:
although there is a multiplicity in the universe of discourse counting sub-
stance and modes as many, this multiplicity is only a set theoretical epiphe-
nomenon of the power set algebra over the model theoretic picture of "sub-
stance", which is but defined as the set theoretical union of the modes, thus
the monist stance remains untouched by the "de deo" disjunctions.

Now, my further suggestion, that the answer found for the model theoretic
pictures of "substance, as perceived by an attribute" and "mode, as
perceived by an attribute" - by inverse mapping - applies as well for the
question wrt the original concepts "de deo", relies on the conviction that the
model theoretic picture of modes, as drawn via a specific semantic definition,
descending from the model theoretic picture of attribute preserves metaphys-
ical structure.
There can’t be a direct proof of this kind of adequacy. Eventually the whole
reconstructional enterprise need be judged a) from the consistency of
it premisses, and b) from the fertility of its application.

5.4.1
digression on "identity" theses

The neither clause above comments on explications of attribute, which pro-
mote some kind of respective "identity thesis", e.g. Shein [11] [sections 1.4 and
1.9.3] takes it for granted, that Spinoza in "de deo" asserts this identity, and
as evidence for this he cites from e1p4d, e1p19, e1p20c2. To be fair, neither
e1p19 nor e1p20c2 contain anything decisive to such an identity thesis, but
we need have a closer look at a critical clause in e1p4d. The complete text
passage reads:

"Propositio IV. Duae aut plures res distinctae vel inter se distinguuntur ex
diversitate attributorum substantiarum,vel ex diversitate earundem affectionum.
Demonstratio. Omnia, quae sunt, vel in se, vel in alio sunt (per axiom. 1.),
hoc est (per defin. 3. et 5.), extra intellectum nihil datur praeter substantias
earumque affectiones. Nihil ergo extra intellectum datur, per quod plures res
distingui inter se possunt praeter substantias sive, quod idem est (per defin. 4.),
earum attributa earumque affectiones. Q. e. d." e1p4 - e1p4d [13], p. [47-48]

and extract the critical clause:
... per quod plures res distingui inter se possunt praeter substantias sive, quod
idem est (per defin. 4.), earum attributa earumque affectiones ...

Well, obviously Shein reads this idem as relating to substantias and earum
attributa, in which case one but would expect not idem est, but idem sunt.

My reading - instead - of this clause goes:
... quod plures res distingui inter se praeter substantias ... idem est ... ... quod
plures res distingui inter se praeter substantiae attributas ...
[bc attributes
express their respective essences]

I take such identity theses, of Shein’s or whatever other coinage, to be not
only non-explicative wrt e1d4 and therefore question begging, but the more
from an epistemological p.o.v., to commit a category mistake. For a related
category mistake see the closing section of [7].

15

X has at his command at one hand
Y1 = a special kinematic theory (viz., a theory that covers metric trajectory

"The orbit of every planet is an ellipse with the sun at one of the two foci."[15],
p.3

and further X has at his command a set of verified astronomical data Y2 =
a set of "time tables" of planet positions [relative to the sun],

and X concludes from these sources Y1 and Y2 that they fit the hypothesis
that Kepler’s second law applies to the local solar system. And is ready to ac-
cept a generalization, that this kinematic law will apply to other solar systems
of like pattern as well.

thus, accepting Kepler’s first and second law, X acquires a rather clear and
distinct idea of an infinite mode of planetary motion as a facet of the idea of
the aeternal and infinite mode of motion and rest, which is the attribute of
extension.
