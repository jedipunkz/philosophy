During  and  previous  years  to  the  Revolution,  the  Indian  Tribes  inhabiting
southern  Mlchlglan,  Ohio  and  western  Pennsylvania,  had  indulged  very  ex¬
tensively  the  habit  of  capturing  white  children  and  youth,  for  adoption  into
there  tribes.  Though  in  to  many  cases  the  captives  fell  victims  to  tomahawks,  #
impelled  by  capricious  tempers,  while  but  a  few  comparatively  were  spared  to
effort  their  escape  to  civilization.  These  escapes  were  sometimes  effected
by  stealth  and  sometimes  by  purchase,  by  a  man  trading  furs  and  articles  of
barter  for  the  release  of  these  victims.

Most  pioneers  came  from  Pennsylvania  to  Colchester  and  the  area. The  progenitor
of  the  Canadian  branch  of  the  Her  family  was  named  Jacob,  who  removed  from
southern  Pennsylvania  to  Grosse  Isle  in  1790*  there  remained  till  1808,

when  he  became  a  settler  in  Colchester  on  lots  38  &  39 »  which  have  remained
in  the  possession  of  members  of  the  family  until  recently  sold.  On  his
arrival  his  family  included  his  son  David,  Solomon,  John,  the  latter  who  is
still  living  with  a  son  in  the  locality  of  his  orginal  settlement.  This
family  has  been  closely  identified  with  the  improvement  and  development  of
this  section  of  Colchester,  and  at  least  one  son  of  the  gentleman  last  named,
Mr  John  Iler,  now  residing  on  lot  45  Lake  Shore,  has  been  long  and  creditabily
connected  with  the  administration  of  muncipal  affairs  in  the  township, Filling
the  Reeveship  during  a  long  successive  period,  and  serving  one  year  in  the
Warden*  s  chair,
