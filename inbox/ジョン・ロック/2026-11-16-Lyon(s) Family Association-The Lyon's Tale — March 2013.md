From the President

It’s always a special day when you get a phone call or a letter
from a cousin that you never knew. You know what I’m talking
about. One day you are sitting with all your family records
scattered around you and you are wondering how to make sense
of any of it. Then that new contact is made. Someone that you
never knew existed has just found your information in one of
the Lyon’s Tale newsletters or online at a site such as

Ancestry.com They inquire about one of the people in your family tree – and
suddenly you have found a new cousin. OK, maybe it’s a 5th cousin 3 times
removed, but a cousin nonetheless. A blood relative. And the best part is –
they’ve got family information that you’ve been seeking for years. Today’s
technology helps this sort of connection occur with increasing frequency. It assists
us in our study of family history and continues to raise our efforts to new heights.

And where does an organization like the Lyon(s) Families Association fit into
today’s scheme of things? We began in 1951 with a small number of people with a
common interest in genealogy. They pooled their records, copied files and notes
from research libraries and old bibles. They compiled, in the pre-computer age,
large folders of loose sheet documentation and spent untold hours comparing
names, dates and locations to create a skeleton framework of the network of
families that today comprise the Lyon clan. Today, those records exist digitally at
the LFA and, with modern computer software, permit instantaneous searches and
records updates to occur as these new cousins are made known. Undoubtedly, the
huge Lyon(s) database that we maintain is the most complete set of (largely US)
Lyon family linkages anywhere in the world.

That’s pretty impressive as far as it goes, but where do we go from here? Well, I
believe we must look to our origins. Just as those individuals pooled their records,
we must ALL contribute to the growth of the organization. Each of you has many
records that you’ve collected. PLEASE send a copy of those records to us. Don’t
simply assume that we already have all the information that you do. You may
possess a key piece of information that no one else has found. Your continued
contributions to the LFA will ensure our viability and utility as we move forward.

Mike

From the editor:

Dear cousins,

After seeing the very fine recent Steven Spielberg film Lincoln, Rick and I finally got around to
reading Doris Kearns Goodwin's equally fine book, Team of Rivals, which is one of Spielberg's
major source texts for the film. We've had the book for quite awhile, but we picked it up just
recently.

The book contains a wonderful Civil War anecdote about the LFA's great hero, Brig. Gen. Nathaniel
Lyon. After he stopped the rebels from capturing the arsenal at St. Louis, the general decided to
scout the Confederate encampment at Fort Jackson MO. Lyon actually went into the encampment
and got valuable information about the layout and troops which allowed him to return the next day
with his forces and capture the fort.

How did he do it? He disguised himself as "a well-respected old lady in St. Louis," who was a
frequent visitor to the camp. The general's friend, Frank Blair, apparently supplied his mother-in-
law's dress. His red beard was hidden by a "thickly veiled sunbonnet." Thus dressed, the general
had access without hindrance to the camp; however, just in case he was found out, he carried pistols
in his egg basket.

*****
*****

There is an oddly humorous story from the Baker side of my family which also involves cross-
dressing. A woman named Vera Hartman Witte of Freeport IL (which was founded by my ancestor
William "Tutty" Baker, to whom Vera was also related) was unfairly divorced by her abusive
husband, and left without means. After the final decree, on June 9, 1931, she accosted her now-ex-
husband on the courthouse steps and shot him dead! Of course, she was captured immediately,
convicted in a coroner’s inquest, and sent to state prison at Joliet.

One day two men visited Vera in her cell. After awhile, the men asked to be let out, and they went
on their way. Several hours later, the guard delivered a supper tray to Vera -- but it wasn't Vera in
the cell! One of the men had changed clothes with her, and Vera had walked out of the jail with the
other man and disappeared, never to be seen in those parts again. I don't know what happened to the
two men who conspired with her; but Vera became a folk hero around Freeport, as she was far more
popular than her no-good ex.

I've "rescued" two old photographs of Hattie LOCKE CARTWRIGHT and Cora LOCKE LYONS.

1930) and together they had two sons, Wilbur E. and Everett M.

Milestones

John J. Lyons, Sr., 94, of Thomaston CT and Venice, FL, died Friday morning, January 4, 2013, at
his home in Thomaston, three days after the passing of his beloved wife Margaret "Peg"
(Hennessey) Lyons. John and Peg would have celebrated their 69th wedding anniversary on July 1.
Mr. Lyons was born Feb. 19, 1918 in Thomaston, son of the late William H. and Mary (Carson)
Lyons. He was a graduate of Crosby High School in Waterbury, the College of the Holy Cross in
Worcester, MA and the McAllister School of Embalming in New York and the U.S. Coast Guard
Academy Reserve Course in New London. He served as a lieutenant during World War II in the
North Atlantic and the European Theater.

He had served as Chairman and member of the Board of Directors of the Terryville Trust Co., a
member of the board of the New Britain Bank and Trust Co., a member of the Connecticut Bank
Federation in Hartford, and was a Corporator of the Thomaston Savings Bank. He was also a
member of the Litchfield County University Club, was a trustee and communicant of St. Thomas
Church in Thomaston and a communicant of the Epiphany Cathedral in Venice, FL, and was a
former member of the Chippanee Golf Club in Bristol and the Mission Valley Country Club in Laurel,
FL.

Active in Thomaston, he had served as Project Coordinator for the Thomaston High School, was a
member and Grand Knight in the Knights of Columbus Atlantic Council #18, a charter member of
the Thomaston Stag Club, a charter member of the Thomaston Rotary Club, and a member of the
Board of Finance for 33 years. He had served as a representative of the Boston Pontiac Zone on
the Eastern Regional Council and was a member of the Connecticut Automobile Dealers
Association. He operated the automobile dealership, school bus company, and funeral home in
Thomaston for many years.

He is survived by his children Paul Lyons of Surprise, AZ; Jay Lyons of Farmington; Mary Ellen
Lyons of Brookfield; Martha Lyons-Holden of Ft. Myers, FL; Peter Lyons of Scottsdale, AZ; and
Molly Fisher of Dudley, MA; his sister, Rosemary Martin of Thomaston; eight grandchildren, two
great-grandchildren, and many nieces and nephews. Arrangements are in the care of the Lyons
Funeral Home of Thomaston, www.lyonsfuneralhome.com.

Oral History "Memory Triggers" -- part 3

[Editor's note: This material was sent by LFA President Mike Lyon. Parts 1 and 2 were published
in the June and September 2012 issue of LT. This is the final installment.]

I learned too late that there were many questions I wanted to ask my parents. By the time I began
my search, the sources of the answers were no longer around. There just never seemed to be enough
time, or the proper setting to probe into those more personal aspects of family members’ lives.
Some of what I have learned about my family tree has been through long hours spent researching
books, census records and the like. But these are mere dates, locations, and names. The legends,
stories, feelings and thoughts and memories of these ancestors are lost forever. Below you will see
questions I’ve collected over the years -- questions I wish I had thought of to ask my relatives when
they were still alive. You’ll find some of these to be repetitive or similar. I’m leaving these in the
list; they may – coming from different directions – prompt slightly different or expanded responses.
Feel free to use them as you interview the older generation. Feel even freer to record your own
responses for those who will come later. Believe me, they will thank you for it!
