The puzzle

Why the most durable form of ownership ever built rests on the flimsiest-seeming fiction
of all.

be a fiction — a made thing, an anchor, a story about who rightfully holds

None of these is a fact of nature. Each is a robe woven over a distribution of holdings
that force originally seized, and the robe works not because it is true but because its
author sits beyond the reach of anyone who might tear it off.

Modern property feels different. When a person today owns a house, the
ownership does not seem to rest ona story at all. It rests on the Jaw — on a title deed,
a registry, a court that will enforce the boundary, a state that stands behind the whole
apparatus. This feels solid in a way that "I descend from Solomon" does not; it feels
less like a fiction and more like a fact. And yet it is a fiction, in exactly the sense this
series means: "this land is mine, and will be mine when I sleep and when I die and
when the government changes, and others will treat my heir as its owner" is a claim
about the future behaviour of other people, not a fact of nature, and it has to be
authored by something. The puzzle is that this particular fiction — impersonal law
securing private property — has proven the most durable and the most expansive
form of ownership ever devised, underwriting the wealthiest and most powerful
societies in history and a global empire besides. Why should the fiction that feels /east
like a fiction be the strongest one? What is its author, and why is that author so

much harder to tear down than a god or a bloodline or a king?

This study answers by tracing how the fiction was built, in England, across three
moves and a hundred and seventy years. Henry VII relocated the author of English
title from a foreign person, the Pope, to a domestic institution, the Crown-in-
Parliament. The Glorious Revolution of 1688 settled which half of that institution
was sovereign, and made the author fully impersonal — a procedure no single person
could revise. And John Locke, rationalising the settlement after the fact, relocated
the w/timate author one final step, out of every human institution altogether and
into nature itself, so that property no longer needed any authorising person, only a
law that recognised a title nature had already granted. Each move made the anchor
harder to forge by making its author harder to seize — from a person you could defy,
to an institution you could not capture, to a natural fact you could not even reach.

The strongest fiction is the one whose author is, in the end, nobody at all.

The Henrician break (1530s). Henry VIII, king of England, needing a
male heir and blocked by the Pope from annulling his first marriage, removed
England from the authority of the Roman Church. Through a sequence of statutes
in the 1530s, above all the Act of Supremacy of 1534, Parliament declared the king
the supreme head of the Church of England, and between 1536 and 1540 the
Crown dissolved the monasteries — which held perhaps a fifth to a quarter of the
cultivated land of England — and sold most of that land, cheaply and widely, to
nobles, gentry, and merchants. The break was grounded not merely in Henry's will
but in a claim about scripture and the ancient church, and it was enacted through
Parliament, whose statutes now carried the supremacy.

The Stuart conflict (1603-1660). In 1603 the throne passed to the Stuart
dynasty, who inherited the composite authority Henry had left — king together
with Lords and Commons in Parliament — but who tried to answer, in the crown's
favour, a question Henry had never had to settle: when king and Parliament disagree,
who is sovereign? The early Stuarts asserted a divine right of kings — the doctrine
that the king's authority came directly from God and was answerable to God alone,
not to Parliament. This claim, that the king was the sole author of law and
Parliament merely his advisor, collided with a Parliament that claimed its own
consent was necessary for taxation and law. The collision became civil war in the
1640s; Parliament's forces won; and in 1649 King Charles I was tried and beheaded
by his own subjects — an event without precedent, a king killed not by a rival

claimant but by the institution that claimed to stand above him. The republic that

Locke (1689-90). In 1689-90, in the wake of the Revolution, John Locke

he was secretary to the Lords Proprietors of the colony of Carolina, helped draft its

England's slave trade, and served on the body that administered the colonies. His

theory and his colonial career were of a piece, in ways this study will not pass over.

Read through the framework, 1688 is the second relocation, and it finishes the
sentence Henry began. Henry had moved the author of English title from a foreign
person into a domestic composite institution but left unsettled which part of that
institution was sovereign. The Bill of Rights settles it: the law is not the king's to

now, unambiguously, the King-77-Parliament with Parliament as the senior partner

even the king, can revise it, because it takes the whole legislative instrument
(Commons, Lords, and crown together) to change what the whole legislative
instrument made. The author of English title has become a procedure rather than a
person — and a procedure, like Venice's unforgeable lottery, cannot be captured by
any one competitor because there is no single seat to seize. This is the framework's
deepest requirement met in a new way: not a god beyond the world, not a bloodline
no one can forge, but an institution no one can control alone. The monarch has
become, constitutionally, what an earlier settlement had made him ecclesiastically —
a governor who stands beneath the anchor rather than serving as it.

And the settlement was, for the propertied, exactly the trade the framework has
seen made before. The English elite gave up the prize of being the author — the
chance for any one of them, or the king, to hold the realm's title in his personal gift

Their property no longer rested on controlling the crown, and therefore they no
longer had to fight endlessly to control it; their titles rested on a law that held for all
of them at once, secured by an author no single hand could move. It is the Venetian
patriciate’s bargain and the American founders’ bargain: submit to a source of title
no competitor can seize, and be safe from every competitor in exchange. The endless
factional struggle to own the author — which is what politics is when the author is a
person — gave way to a settled distribution under an author that belonged to no

one.

The move is made in the fifth chapter of the Second Treatise, and its logic is worth
following exactly, because it is the hinge of the modern world's intuitions about
ownership. Locke begins from self-ownership: every man, he writes, has a property
in his own person, to which no one else has any right. From this the labour of a
man's body and the work of his hands are his own. And therefore, in his central
formula: whatsoever a man removes out of the state that nature has provided and left
it in, he has mixed his labour with, and joined to it something that is his own, and
thereby makes it his property — for this labour, being the unquestionable property of
the labourer, excludes the common right of other men. Property, on this account, is
not created by a grant from a king, or a bull from a Pope, or a statute of Parliament.
It is created by the individual, through labour, out of nature, before any government
exists — and government arises only afterward, by the consent of property-holders,
for the purpose of protecting what nature and labour had already made theirs.

Read through the framework, this is the most radical relocation of the three, and
the most powerful. Consider where each prior anchor located the author of
property. Under the medieval scheme, title flowed downward through persons: God
to Pope to king to subject, each an author who could, in principle, be defied,

deposed, or beheaded. Henry cut the top link and lodged the author in an

This is the resolution of the study's puzzle. The reason modern property feels less

exists nowhere cannot be captured by anyone. That is its strength. It is also, as we will

Revolution of 1688 proved this in practice before Locke rationalised it: England
changed dynasties — swapped James for William and Mary — without a single
landholder's title coming into question, because no title had rested on that specific
king in the first place. The framework's most dangerous failure mode, the
indeterminate succession, is simply switched off. Ownership is unhooked from the

one event that used to threaten it most.

The second is that Locke's move ends the factional war that the framework

Fundamental Constitutions, which granted every freeman "absolute power and
authority" over his slaves and erected a hereditary colonial aristocracy. He held shares
connected to the Royal African Company, the corporation that ran England's
Atlantic slave trade, shipping enslaved Africans to the plantations of the very
colonies he helped administer. The scholarship debates the precise degree and
character of his investment — some shares appear to have come as compensation for
his colonial offices rather than as direct speculation — but the entanglement is not in
doubt: the philosopher who located the author of property in the labouring
individual's natural right was, in his working life, an administrator and beneficiary of
an empire that expropriated the labour of enslaved people and the land of
dispossessed ones. The theory and the empire were not in tension. They were the
same project. The labour theory of property is, read from the inside, the charter of
human freedom and secure ownership; read from the outside — from the position
of the Powhatan, the Cherokee, the enslaved African whose own labour was owned
by another — it is the charter of the largest expropriation in human history, and it
required no god's blessing to operate, because it had made nature itself the author of
the taking.

This is the framework's darkest law in its purest instance. The anchor that most
completely secured the inside — by making the author of property a natural fact

licensed the outside, because a title authored by nature could be claimed by whoever
performed the labour nature was said to reward, against anyone whose different
relationship to the land did not count as labour at all. The strength and the horror
