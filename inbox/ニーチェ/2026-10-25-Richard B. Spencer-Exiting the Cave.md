Exiting the Cave
New Courses at ALEX University
2022-11-18

Dear Friends,

We will, of course, continue talking about politics and the passing scene in this group. But, like many of you, I feel this is, at the end of the day, unsatisfying. It’s lacking something. It’s passive and doesn’t foster growth.

This is why I started ALEX university. Yes, it is a small, reasonable project: classes are six-weeks long; all courses are $100; and diplomas are fit for framing (Lol). But it’s also the most important project I’ve ever launched.

We won’t be tackling Plato’s Republic quite yet (wait until 2023), but the selection above gives you a sense of the environment of I’m promoting: close reading, attention to detail, dialectic, and the unrelenting search for truth.

ALEX can be scaled in exciting ways in the future. I can imagine these sessions occurring in person and in our own facility. Most everyone is in agreement that the current university system isn't serving its primary function: it is wildly expensive, increasingly silly and pointless, and its admirable qualities are “wasted on the young.” ALEX doesn’t replace the current system so much as offer a different direction altogether.

It’s worth noting that this direction isn't “back to basics” or rudimentary education. (That’s a worthy endeavor but something I leave up to others). If you are signing up for these courses, it’s assumed that you are intelligent and serious. ALEX is about advancing a school of thought and way of seeing the world. This is centered on Apolloism, as formulated by Mark Brahmin, and it radiates outward from there. In other words, we come from a perspective, and one that is illuminated by examining these important texts.

I’m proud to offer two new courses in the immediate future: “The Superman” (December 4, 2022 through January 15, 2023), which offers a close reading of Nietzsche’s Thus Spoke Zarathustra, and the second edition of The Symbolic Language of Hollywood Cinema (December 3 through January 15), which focuses on science- fiction movies.

Here are the prospectuses for each course.

The Superman:

More than century after his death, Friedrich Nietzsche (1844-1900) remains one of the world’s most popular, notorious, and misunderstood philosophers. This is especially true of his most famous work, Thus Spoke Zarathustra (1883–1892), which includes dramatic action, evokes ancient religion, resembles the Christian Gospels, and combines both bombast and self-deprecating irony. Nietzsche appears to be launching a new religion, though, according to the author himself, one that is available only to a chosen few. We will strive to make sense of all this through close reading.

This is a six-week, twelve-hour course based around interpreting Thus Spoke Zarathustra. Themes include Nietzsche’s account of the “Death of God”; the Übermensch (Overman or Superman), the Eternal Recurrence of the Same, the self-destruction of Western civilization., and Nietzsche's conception of religion.

Enroll

The Symbolic Language of Hollywood Cinema:

In this course, we will re-view iconic works of Hollywood cinema through the lens of REM Theory, developed by Mark Brahmin. The focus will be science fiction. A central claim of REM Theory is that an international symbolic language exists, which has been passed down and developed over centuries, and which inflects works as varied as the the Bible, Medieval mythology, and contemporary popular culture. Salient works of Hollywood movie-making are thus not simply forms of art and entertainment but carry messages, delivered unconsciously to the audience (the “people in the dark”). Cinema thus serves a religious function in a technological society, and one of psychological and political control.

Enroll

All sessions are recorded, and the audio will be posted shortly after completion. So, yes, you can purchase the course and “audit” it via listening to the session afterwards. But that just wouldn’t be the same as participating, now would it?

I’m intensely excited for both courses. You simply cannot experience this kind of education anywhere else. And I encourage you to enroll.

Sincerely,

Richard
