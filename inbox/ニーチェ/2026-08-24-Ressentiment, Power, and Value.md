## Abstract

<p>This paper presents an interpretation of the attitude of life-denial as an extreme form of <italic>ressentiment</italic> and offers a sketch of the role played by <italic>ressentiment</italic> in the genesis of life-denial. In particular, it is suggested that identifying <italic>ressentiment</italic> as the source of the ascetic ideal enables Nietzsche to explain how it can both ‘seduce’ and yet end up being a life-denying force. The adherent to the ascetic ideal is ‘inept at <italic>living</italic>’ and in particular at securing the satisfaction of their most basic natural needs. This inability to satisfy one’s most basic needs not only arouses <italic>ressentiment</italic> but also elicits a feeling of impotence. The paper advances the view that the negative valuation of what is natural and instinctual, or the ends the agent feels unable to realize, is motivated by <italic>ressentiment</italic> and aims at restoring in him the ‘feeling of power’ that has been injured by his repeated failures.</p>
