Two New Courses at ALEX U
On Nietzsche and Cinema
2022-10-15

Dear All,

I’m very happy to announce that ALEX University is starting up again in November. Today, I’m announcing two new courses, taught by Mark Brahmin and myself. (Tomorrow, I’ll be announcing three more, stretching into 2023.).

The first in on Nietzsche’s enigmatic masterwork, Thus Spoke Zarathustra.

More than century after his death, Friedrich Nietzsche (1844-1900) remains one of the world’s most popular, notorious, and misunderstood philosophers. This is especially true of his most famous work, Thus Spoke Zarathustra (1883–1892), which includes dramatic action, evokes ancient religion, resembles the Christian Gospels, and combines both bombast and self-depricating irony. Nietzsche appears to be launching a new religion, though, according to the author himself, one that is understandle only to a chosen few. We will strive to make sense of all this through close reading.

This is a five-week, ten-hour course based around interpreting Thus Spoke Zarathustra. Themes include Nietzsche’s account of the “Death of God”; the Übermensch (Overman or Superman), the Eternal Recurrence of the Same, self-destruction of Western civilization, and Nietzsche's conception of religion.

This will take place on Sundays, from November 6 to December 4, 2022.

Enroll

The second course is a continuation of Mark’s and my REM Cinema series. This time, we’ll focus on science fiction!

In this course, we will re-view iconic works of Hollywood cinema through the lens of REM Theory, developed by Mark Brahmin. The focus will be science fiction. A central claim of REM Theory is that an international symbolic language exists, which has been passed down and developed over centuries, and which inflects works as varied as the the Bible, Medieval mythology, and contemporary popular culture. Salient works of Hollywood movie-making are thus not simply forms of art and entertainment but carry messages, delivered unconsciously to the audience, the “people in the dark.” Cinema thus serves a religious function in technological society, and one of social and political control.

This will take place on Saturdays, from November 5 to December 3, 2022.

Enroll

Some notes…

I learned some important things in our inaugural “semester” this past summer.

All courses will be once a week for five weeks. We often go over the allotted class time, and both Mark and I would be happy to add an extra week for discussion if the class is interested. However, we both want these sessions to be focused and intense.

I used Keynote/Powerpoint in one class, but not the other. Many students liked the structure that a presentation brings, so I’ll be doing that for all courses, and the “deck” will be made available afterwards.

Enrolling automatically gives you access to our Members areas. This is where you can get the Zoom link and recorded audio from each session. Additionally, I’m going to give you access to this summer’s sessions, on Nietzsche and REM in Cinema, so you’ll be able to brush up.

No writing assignments this time; instead, participants are encouraged to offer brief oral presentations on the readings.

Coming soon…

We have a few more courses in the works, which I’ll announce on Sunday. I’ll be leading a close reading of Plato in two courses. Also, Mark and I will offer a course on REM proper, after Mark’s book is published in January, 2023.

Thank you for joining us on this journey!

Best wishes,

Richard
