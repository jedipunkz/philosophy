## Abstract

ABSTRACT
                  
                    Frege understands assertion as a bipartite act consisting of the indication of a content plus the presentation of that content as true. This allows a straightforward account of the unasserted proposition: in an assertion ‘~
                    p
                    ’, the symbol ‘
                    p
                    ’ indicates a content that is not here presented as true. Geach and Kimhi reject Frege's bipartite picture, maintaining that the symbol ‘
                    p
                    ’ of an assertion ‘~
                    p
                    ’ is the same symbol as the ‘
                    p
                    ’ which, occurring by itself, is an assertion that
                    p
                    . Where it occurs within a larger logical whole, they however suggest, the symbol's assertoric function is disrupted so that no assertion is made that
                    p
                    . This paper argues that neither account can be attributed to Wittgenstein's
                    Tractatus
                    . Wittgenstein does not begin with a non‐asserting ‘
                    p
                    ’ which may be augmented to an assertion. Nor does he begin with an asserting ‘
                    p
                    ’ which may be diminished to a non‐assertion. Rather, the asserted and unasserted propositions are two distinct symbols.
