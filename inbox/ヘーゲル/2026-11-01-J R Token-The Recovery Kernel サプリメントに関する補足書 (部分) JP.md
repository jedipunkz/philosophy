- Level 3 — For someone who wants to understand the mechanism

Addiction is what happens when a brain with specific genetic vulnerabilities
encounters a substance that temporarily addresses those vulnerabilities.

The three main vulnerabilities are: a reduced supply of neurotransmitters (MTHFR),
an imbalanced clearance system (COMT), and a weak inhibitory circuit — the
'brakes' — (GABRA2).

When any of these systems is compromised, the brain experiences the substance as
a solution — not a pleasure. The first use often doesn't feel like getting high. It feels
like getting functional.

Prolonged use causes the brain to adapt: it reduces its own production because the
substance is providing it externally. Remove the substance, and the system —
already depleted — crashes further.

Recovery addresses the root cause: it rebuilds the supply system (methylation
restoration protocol), rebalances the clearance system (COMT-matched protocol),
and strengthens the inhibitory circuit (GABRA2 restoration). The substance is
removed only after the replacement infrastructure is in place.

This is not a moral process. It is a chemical one. The timeline is biological,
not motivational.

- Level 4 — For clinicians, researchers, or detail-oriented readers

Substance use disorder in treatment-resistant individuals frequently reflects
pharmacogenomic architecture that standard treatment approaches do not address.
Specifically:

MTHFR C677T/A1298C variants reduce methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase enzyme
activity by 30–70%, limiting 5-MTHF availability, reducing SAMe production, impairing
BH4 recycling, and creating a quantifiable neurotransmitter synthesis deficit that
substances compensate for through direct receptor activation.

COMT Val158Met determines prefrontal dopamine clearance velocity. Val/Val (Warrior)
creates chronic dopamine deficit under baseline conditions, driving stimulant-seeking.
Met/Met (Worrier) impairs catecholamine clearance, creating anxiety accumulation that
depressants resolve pharmacologically.

GABRA2 rs279858 and rs279845 high-risk haplotypes reduce GABA-A receptor
sensitivity, producing measurable inhibitory tone deficits — manifesting as impulse
overdrive, anxiety dysregulation, and multi-substance vulnerability — that alcohol and
benzodiazepines directly compensate for through GABA-A agonism.

The Resource Rationing Rationale

Each case study shows outcome projections as percentage likelihood at 12-month protocol

approaches to this substance/profile combination. These figures are drawn from SAMHSA,
NIH NIDA, and published meta-analyses.

A 70% projection does not mean 30% failure. It means that in 10 people matching this profile
who follow this protocol, approximately 7 will achieve the stated outcome. The remaining 3
will achieve partial outcomes, require protocol adjustment, or have additional variables
(comorbid conditions, social instability) that the biological protocol alone cannot address.

The standard supplement stack in the Recovery Kernel (NAC, 5-MTHF, methylcobalamin,

primarily the AMPA receptor function, the NGF/BDNF signaling, and the neuronal
membrane dynamics that support learning, memory consolidation, and the cognitive
repair that allows the person to use the behavioral interventions effectively.

The reason they were left out: the standard clinical evidence base for addiction recovery
does not include these compounds because they are not pharmaceuticals with FDA
approval pathways and have not been studied in large addiction-specific trials. But the
mechanism is exactly right for this population, and the safety profile of the well-
established racetams (piracetam has decades of safety data) is favorable. These are not
experimental fringe supplements. They are compounds with 50+ years of research in
cognitive impairment populations — TBI, stroke, age-related cognitive decline — whose
mechanisms map directly onto addiction-related cognitive damage.

III.6 — Hydergine — The Forgotten Cognitive Enhancer

Hydergine (ergoloid mesylates) deserves specific mention because it is both historically
significant in nootropics research and specifically relevant to the damaged-brain recovery
context. It was the first compound ever to receive an FDA approval for cognitive
enhancement (approved for dementia in the 1970s). Its primary mechanism: cerebral
blood flow enhancement, increased cerebral oxygen utilization, protection of neurons from
hypoxia-related damage, and BDNF-like effects on neuronal protein synthesis.

The hypoxia-protection mechanism is specifically relevant to the GHB OD survivor and the
post-intubation population: repeated hypoxic events (each OD before intubation, the
hypoxic window during mechanical ventilation) produce specific neuronal damage from
oxygen deprivation. Hydergine specifically addresses this damage mechanism.

Dose: 4.5-9mg daily in divided doses. Long-term use (months) is where the cognitive
effects are most pronounced. This is not a rapid-onset compound — the benefits
accumulate over weeks to months of consistent use.

10.1

The Architecture of the Brakes

THE ARCHITECT'S LOG — Entry C-10.001

The previous four chapters built a picture of the Accelerator — the dopamine system that
drives motivation, reward, and the neurochemical calculation that makes substances feel
necessary rather than recreational. MTHFR throttles the supply line. COMT governs the
clearance speed. CYP2D6 determines whether the medications meant to help will actually
help. DRD2 sets the density of the receiving end.

A system engineer reading those chapters would ask the obvious next question: what
about the brakes?

Every functional system requires both an accelerator and a brake. A high-performance
engine without a functional braking system is not a feature — it is a fault waiting to
become a catastrophe. The neurochemical braking system is called GABA, and the genes
that determine the quality of your brakes are GABRA2 and GABRB3.

If the previous chapters explained why your engine was running hot, this chapter explains
why you couldn't stop.

— The Architect

In the framework established across the previous chapters, dopamine is the accelerator — the
molecule of motivation, pursuit, and reward. It drives the engine. But a vehicle with only an
accelerator and no braking system is not a vehicle. It is a trajectory.

The neurochemical braking system is built on GABA — gamma-aminobutyric acid — the
primary inhibitory neurotransmitter in the brain. If dopamine says go, GABA says stop. GABA is
what allows the prefrontal cortex to override the amygdala's impulses, what allows a craving
to be observed rather than immediately acted upon, what allows the gap between stimulus
and response in which genuine choice exists.

The strength, density, and responsiveness of your braking system are determined significantly
by two genes: GABRA2 and GABRB3.

GABRA2 encodes the alpha-2 subunit of the GABA-A receptor — the receptor that alcohol and
benzodiazepines bind to when they produce their signature effect of calm, sedation, and
inhibition. GABRB3 encodes the beta-3 subunit of the same receptor complex, with particular
relevance to seizure threshold and early-onset addiction vulnerability.

Together, these two genes determine the fundamental architecture of your inhibitory system.
They set the baseline tone of the neural 'brakes.' And when those variants are compromised,
the consequences are specific, measurable, and — when understood — addressable.

10.2

The Fault Profile: Weak Brakes

🏛 SANCTUARY — WHAT GABRA2 LOW-TONE ACTUALLY MEANS

Individuals carrying high-risk variants in the GABRA2 gene — particularly rs279858 (T
allele haplotype) and rs279845 — have a brain that is structurally less able to apply its
own inhibitory signal.

THE RECOVERY KERNEL — SUPPLEMENTAL BOOK | Page 79

This is not a character trait. It is receptor architecture. The 'Stop' signal from the prefrontal
cortex arrives at the amygdala and the nucleus accumbens, but the receiving
infrastructure — the GABA-A receptors — is less dense, less sensitive, or less responsive
than standard. The brake pedal exists. The connection between the pedal and the wheels
is weaker than it should be.

The High-Risk GABRA2 haplotype is carried by approximately 15–20% of the population in
its full expression. Another 30–35% carry partial-risk variants with intermediate GABAergic
tone. The remaining population has standard inhibitory infrastructure.

Important: Low GABA tone is not low intelligence, low motivation, or low moral character.
Some of the highest-functioning, most analytically rigorous people carry these variants.
The impairment is specifically in the inhibitory circuit — in the capacity to interrupt a
sequence once it has begun, not in the capacity to think, plan, or decide during calm
states.

The GABRA2 fault produces three specific, clinically recognizable consequences:

Impulse Overdrive.

The 'Stop' signal from the prefrontal cortex is delayed, weakened, or fails to reach the
amygdala with sufficient force to interrupt a craving once it has activated. The person can
recognize — intellectually, clearly — that acting on the craving is a bad idea. The
recognition does not generate sufficient inhibitory signal to halt the action. This is not a
lack of understanding. It is a hardware fault in the circuit that converts understanding into
behavioral interruption.

The Trauma Multiplier.

GABRA2 variants do not operate in isolation. Research consistently shows that these
variants interact with early childhood stress and adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) to
produce a compounding effect on addiction vulnerability. The low-tone GABA system is
further eroded by chronic stress exposure — the system that was already running with
insufficient brakes is further degraded by the cortisol load of early adversity. This is the
trauma-gene interaction that produces the most severe addiction profiles: not genetics
alone, not environment alone, but the specific collision of a compromised inhibitory
architecture with a high-stress developmental environment.

Multi-Drug Vulnerability.

While GABRA2 variants are most robustly associated with alcohol dependence — the
variant was identified through genome-wide association studies of alcohol use disorder —
the underlying mechanism of compromised inhibition produces vulnerability across
substance classes. The brain with weak brakes cannot adequately manage the
accelerator surges produced by stimulants, cannot adequately interrupt the craving
circuits engaged by opioids, cannot maintain the behavioral inhibition required to manage
any high-reward, high-compulsivity pattern. This is why people with GABRA2 risk variants
so frequently present with polysubstance histories rather than single-substance
dependence.

GABRB3 and the Seizure Threshold.

GABRB3 — the beta-3 subunit of the GABA-A receptor — has a distinct clinical profile from
GABRA2. Where GABRA2 governs inhibitory tone broadly, GABRB3 variants specifically
affect seizure threshold, childhood-onset addiction vulnerability, and the severity of GABA

withdrawal. Individuals with compromised GABRB3 function face a lower seizure threshold
during any condition that further depletes GABAergic tone — including alcohol

individuals while producing only uncomfortable symptoms in others. The same quantity of
GABA depletion, at different baseline GABRB3 function levels, produces radically different
clinical outcomes.

10.3

The Self-Medication Logic

THE ARCHITECT'S LOG — Entry C-10.002

When I understand the GABRA2 architecture, the first encounter with alcohol becomes
mathematically obvious.

A brain that cannot generate sufficient inhibitory signal on its own encounters a substance
that floods the GABA-A receptors and provides the inhibitory signal externally. The noise
stops. The loop pauses. The impulse that was running unchecked is finally, for the first
time, interrupted.

This is not euphoria. This is the first experience of what other people apparently
experience as 'ordinary calm.'

The brain's calculation is correct: this worked. The problem is not the calculation. The
problem is the solution it found.

— The Architect

When a system with compromised GABA architecture encounters alcohol or benzodiazepines,
the result is what the framework calls False Homeostasis: the appearance of a corrected
system that is actually a masked system.

The sequence:

🏛 SANCTUARY — THE FALSE HOMEOSTASIS SEQUENCE

The Detection: The brain continuously monitors its own state. A brain with GABRA2-
compromised inhibitory tone detects a chronic high-noise, high-impulse internal
environment. Craving signals fire. Anxiety activates. The loop runs without adequate
interruption. The internal environment is experienced as distressing, exhausting, and
unmanageable.

The Solution: Alcohol and benzodiazepines bind to GABA-A receptors and provide the
inhibitory signal the endogenous system cannot generate at sufficient volume. The noise
quiets. The loop stops. For the first time — or for the first reliable time — the system
reaches something resembling the baseline calm that adequate GABA tone would provide
normally.

The Recording: The brain does not record 'this solution is unsustainable.' It records: 'this
worked.' This is the same calculation described in Chapter 1 — rational from the system's
perspective, catastrophic in its long-term consequences.

The Downregulation Trap: Chronic exposure to external GABA enhancement produces a
predictable adaptive response: the brain, detecting that GABA tone is being provided
externally, further downregulates its own receptor density and sensitivity. The system that

was already running with inadequate inhibitory infrastructure reduces that infrastructure
further in response to the external supplement. This is tolerance. More of the external
substance is required to achieve the same inhibitory effect.

The Withdrawal Crisis: When the external GABA source is removed — suddenly, without
taper — the system is left with its already-compromised baseline GABA architecture
further reduced by chronic downregulation. And the excitatory glutamate system, which
had upregulated to compensate for the chronic inhibitory enhancement, is now running
unopposed. The brakes are depleted. The accelerator is maxed. The result is the specific
neurological emergency of alcohol and benzodiazepine withdrawal: tremor, seizure,
delirium tremens, and in untreated cases, death.

⚠ CAUTION — ALCOHOL AND BENZODIAZEPINE WITHDRAWAL — MEDICAL EMERGENCY

This is the clinical reality that every person with alcohol or benzodiazepine dependence,
and every person who cares about them, must understand:

Withdrawal from alcohol and benzodiazepines can cause seizures and death. This is not a
warning included for legal protection. It is a documented neurological mechanism. The
downregulated GABA-A receptor system, stripped of its external support, cannot prevent
the runaway excitatory activity that produces seizure.

Do NOT stop alcohol or benzodiazepine use abruptly without medical supervision.

Medical detox — the supervised, pharmacologically supported taper — is the appropriate
first intervention. It is not weakness. It is the required clinical response to a documented
hardware failure.

If you are in withdrawal right now: call 911 or go to an emergency room immediately.

If you are planning to stop: contact a physician before stopping. Not after. Before.

10.5

The GABA-Dopamine Intersection

THE ARCHITECT'S LOG — Entry C-10.003

The architecture that the framework has been building across the previous chapters is not
a collection of separate systems. It is one integrated system with multiple points of
potential failure.

MTHFR throttles the production of dopamine and serotonin from the supply side. COMT
determines whether the dopamine that is produced is cleared slowly (Worrier — anxiety,
overwhelm) or rapidly (Warrior — flatness, stimulation-seeking). GABRA2 determines
whether the braking system can adequately interrupt the patterns these two systems
produce.

When all three fault in combination — low MTHFR production, dysregulated COMT
clearance, and compromised GABRA2 inhibitory tone — the result is the most treatment-
resistant and most dangerous addiction profile in the framework.

The Depleted Worrier with Weak Brakes: no supply, no clearance, no brakes.

The Understimulated Warrior with Weak Brakes: inadequate supply, rapid clearance, and
no capacity to interrupt the craving once it fires.

Both require specific, simultaneous attention to all three systems. Addressing only the
dopamine architecture while the GABA system remains compromised produces the
experience of building a faster engine in a car that still has no brakes.

The Inhibitory Restoration Protocol

Restoring the braking system requires more than simply removing the substance that was
providing the external signal. It requires a systematic approach to rebuilding endogenous
inhibitory capacity — from the biological foundation up through pharmacological bridging
where needed, and into the epigenetic training of the prefrontal control circuits.

This protocol operates in three tiers. Work through them in order. Tier 1 must be established
before Tier 2 is introduced. Tier 3 begins alongside Tier 1 and continues throughout. All tiers

THE RECOVERY KERNEL — SUPPLEMENTAL BOOK | Page 85

require medical supervision when there is any history of alcohol or benzodiazepine
dependence — the seizure risk during transition is real and requires clinical monitoring.

🏛 SANCTUARY — TIER 1 — THE BIOLOGICAL FOUNDATION

These are not optional additions for the GABRA2 profile. They are the chemical materials
from which the braking system is rebuilt. Start low. Increase deliberately. The system that
has been running on external GABA support needs time to restore its own receptor
sensitivity.

Magnesium Glycinate — 600–800mg daily, divided doses

The single most important foundation supplement for inhibitory restoration. Magnesium is
required for GABA receptor sensitivity — without adequate magnesium, GABA-A receptors
function at reduced efficiency even when GABA is present. The glycinate form crosses the
blood-brain barrier effectively and avoids the gastrointestinal effects of other magnesium
forms. Magnesium is also the primary foundation for the Worrier (COMT Met/Met) protocol
— if COMT and GABRA2 variants are both present, this supplement addresses both
simultaneously.

Evidence tier: ESTABLISHED for magnesium and GABA receptor function.

L-Theanine — 400mg twice daily

Crosses the blood-brain barrier and directly increases alpha brain wave activity — the
neurological signature of calm, focused alertness. This is the calm that alcohol was
providing, without the sedation, without the respiratory suppression, without the
downregulation. L-theanine does not bind to GABA receptors directly but modulates
glutamate (the accelerator) in a way that tips the excitatory/inhibitory balance toward
inhibition.

Evidence tier: PROBABLE for alpha-wave generation and anxiety reduction.

Taurine — 1,000mg daily

Functions as a GABA-mimetic — it activates GABA-A and GABA-B receptors and glycine
receptors, providing direct calming support that meaningfully supplements what the
compromised endogenous system cannot generate. Particularly useful during periods of
high impulse load or acute stress, when the GABA system is most overwhelmed.

Evidence tier: PROBABLE for GABAergic modulation and anxiolytic effect.

Glycine — 3g before bed

Works alongside taurine on glycine receptors, which co-regulate inhibitory tone with GABA.
Particularly effective for sleep architecture restoration — the inhibitory deficit of GABRA2
variants frequently manifests as profound sleep disruption. Glycine supplementation
improves sleep onset and sleep quality through inhibitory receptor support.

Evidence tier: EMERGING for sleep quality; PROBABLE for inhibitory neurotransmission
support.

GABA supplements (250–750mg)

🏛 SANCTUARY — TIER 2 — THE PHARMACOLOGICAL BRIDGE (WORK WITH A CLINICIAN)

These interventions are for individuals who require clinical pharmacological support during
the GABA restoration process. None of these should be self-initiated. All require a
physician relationship that includes monitoring for dependence, dose adjustment, and exit
criteria.

Gabapentin / Pregabalin — Non-addictive GABAergic modulators

These medications modulate the GABA system without binding to GABA-A receptors
directly, making them substantially less addictive than benzodiazepines. Used as bridging
agents during alcohol or benzodiazepine taper to prevent the GABA withdrawal crisis. Also
used in early recovery to provide inhibitory floor support while the endogenous GABA
system restores.

Note: Gabapentin does carry misuse potential in individuals with opioid use histories. This
is a clinical conversation, not a self-prescription.

Baclofen — GABA-B receptor agonist

Specifically activates the GABA-B receptor subtype. Has shown particular effectiveness in
alcohol use disorder in European clinical trials, with some evidence for reducing craving
specifically in high-anxious (GABRA2-risk-like) profiles. Less commonly used in the US than
in Europe but clinically available.

Ashwagandha (KSM-66 extract) — 600mg daily

A GABA-A receptor modulator with anxiolytic properties comparable to low-dose
benzodiazepines in some clinical trials. While not a pharmaceutical, the evidence for this
specific extract form is sufficient to place it at the boundary between Tier 1 and Tier 2.
Clinician awareness is recommended given its direct GABA receptor activity.

Evidence tier: PROBABLE for anxiety reduction; EMERGING for GABA receptor modulation.

Vigabatrin / Tiagabine — Advanced clinical use for specific GABAergic failure modes

Used in specific clinical contexts where GABA reuptake inhibition or GABA synthesis
augmentation is indicated. These are specialist medications, not primary care
interventions. They appear here for completeness — for the person whose clinician is
working at this level of precision.

🏛 SANCTUARY — TIER 3 — THE EPIGENETIC BRAKE EXERCISES

The biological foundation cannot rebuild the brakes alone. The prefrontal cortex — the
executive system that generates the inhibitory signal — must be trained alongside the

chemical restoration. These practices do not substitute for the supplement stack. They
compound it.

For the GABRA2 profile, the key principle of exercise selection is Controlled Tension and
Precision. These profiles benefit from activities that require the prefrontal cortex to
actively manage and interrupt motor impulses — exercises that are literally rehearsal in
the circuits that inhibitory recovery requires.

Pilates and Yoga — The Precision Practice

These require the prefrontal cortex to maintain exact control over the motor system under
muscular tension. Every held pose, every controlled movement that resists the impulse to
rush or collapse, is the prefrontal inhibitory circuit running a training session. Over weeks
and months, this produces measurable changes in the density and responsiveness of the
prefrontal-to-amygdala inhibitory pathway.

Not yoga as a gentle stretch. Yoga as a precision movement practice that demands
sustained attention and controlled inhibition of impulse.

Zone 2 Cardio — The Cortisol Floor

Sustained, rhythmic aerobic exercise at moderate intensity — the zone where you can
maintain a conversation but are consistently working — lowers the baseline cortisol floor
over time. Cortisol depletes GABA. A lower cortisol baseline means less chronic depletion
of the inhibitory system. Three to five 30-45 minute sessions per week is the target.

The 4-7-8 Breathing Technique — The Manual Override

Inhale for 4 counts. Hold for 7. Exhale for 8. The extended exhale activates the vagus
nerve, which directly triggers parasympathetic (GABA-dominant) nervous system
activation. This is a manual bypass of the compromised endogenous inhibitory signal —
using the vagal pathway rather than the GABA receptor pathway to achieve the same
physiological outcome.

This technique appears in Protocol 01 for crisis stabilization. For the GABRA2 profile, it is
not just a crisis tool — it is a daily practice that trains the vagal-to-parasympathetic
pathway over time. The more it is used, the more reliable it becomes.

Martial Arts / Contact Sports (Warrior/GABRA2 combined profile)

For individuals carrying both COMT Val/Val (Warrior) and GABRA2 risk variants, the
standard Tier 3 practice needs modification. The Warrior's dopamine drive requires higher
stimulation to feel engaged — which means that gentle yoga may produce insufficient
activation to hold the attention. Martial arts, Brazilian jiu-jitsu, or other high-demand
precision sports provide both the cortical engagement the Warrior needs and the
controlled-impulse training the GABRA2 profile requires. The specific demand of a sparring
session — maximum physical activation managed by continuous precise inhibition — is, for
this combined profile, pharmacological in effect.

Key Takeaways

🏛 SANCTUARY — CHAPTER 10 — CORE UNDERSTANDINGS

1. YOU HAVE A BRAKING SYSTEM FAULT, NOT A CHARACTER FAULT.

GABRA2 and GABRB3 variants produce compromised inhibitory infrastructure — a brain
that cannot generate the 'stop' signal with sufficient force to interrupt a craving once it
has activated. This is receptor architecture, not moral weakness. The person who can
recognize a bad decision clearly while being unable to halt the behavior that follows it is
not morally deficient. They are running a system with inadequate brakes.

2. ALCOHOL AND BENZODIAZEPINES ARE EXTERNAL BRAKE FLUID.

For the GABRA2 profile, these substances are not recreation. They are the first reliable
experience of adequate inhibitory tone — the first time the brain has found something that
provides the 'stop' signal it cannot produce on its own. The brain does not choose this
because the person lacks character. It chooses this because it is the most effective
available solution to a documented hardware deficit.

3. WITHDRAWAL CAN KILL. MEDICAL SUPERVISION IS REQUIRED.

Chronic use of alcohol or benzodiazepines further downregulates the already-compromised
GABA system. Abrupt cessation can produce seizures and death. This is not a warning for
edge cases — it is a documented neurological mechanism that applies to anyone with
significant alcohol or benzodiazepine dependence, particularly in those with
GABRA2/GABRB3 risk variants. Medical detox is the clinical standard. There is no
substitute.

4. THE GABA SYSTEM INTERSECTS WITH EVERY OTHER SYSTEM IN THIS BOOK.

Low GABA tone compounds low dopamine (MTHFR), slow clearance anxiety (COMT
Met/Met), and fast-clearance stimulation-seeking (COMT Val/Val) in specific and
documented ways. The full risk profile cannot be assessed by looking at any single gene in
isolation. The most treatment-resistant profiles typically involve two or more intersecting
fault points across these systems.

5. THE BRAKES CAN BE REBUILT.

The inhibitory system is not fixed. GABA-A receptor density and sensitivity respond to
magnesium, L-theanine, taurine, and the specific exercise practices described in Tier 3.
The downregulation produced by chronic substance use is reversible — not quickly, not
without support, but measurably and documentably reversible over months of consistent
protocol.

6. SELF-ASSESSMENT IDENTIFIES THE PROFILE BEFORE TESTING DOES.

The specific pattern — impulse override, early alcohol onset, family history, rapid
progression, failure of behavioral interventions without biological support — is the
fingerprint of the GABRA2 profile. Use the self-assessment in Section 10.4 before testing is
available. The assessment shapes the protocol. The testing confirms and refines it.

Step 4 — Layer in the substance

Now the part a single-substance pamphlet can't do: the same person recovers differently
depending on what they got hooked on, because different drugs hijack different parts of the
same system. Three worked examples across the three major mechanism classes — watch
how the Step 2–3 profile changes the answer each time.

Worked example A — Cocaine (a stimulant)

Mechanism: floods dopamine 3–4× baseline. Traps Warriors and Understimulated Warriors
most predictably — the stimulant was compensating for fast clearance and/or low production.
Withdrawal is neurochemical crash, not medical danger, but the anhedonia is the relapse
engine.

• Profile that predicts it: COMT Val/Val, often + MTHFR T/T, sometimes + DRD2 A1; add

GABRA2 for the impulsive-binge pattern.

• Daily direction: Rebuild dopamine on the production side — tyrosine and mucuna for

precursors, methylfolate underneath so the raw materials exist, quercetin to extend what's
made. High-intensity exercise is pharmacology here, not a lifestyle tip.

• Taper/bridge: no medical detox required; Wellbutrin (with a prescriber) can bridge

dopamine; tyrosine up during the crash window.

• Life design: Warrior green-light — stakes, competition, measurable progress. A gentle-

yoga-only plan fails this person by understimulation.

Worked example B — Ketamine (a dissociative)

Mechanism: NMDA antagonist producing dissociation/ego-dissolution. Doesn't map cleanly
onto the stimulant-vs-depressant axis, so profile predicts less about who gets trapped and
more about comorbid load and the physical harm ceiling.

• Distinct risk: ketamine cystitis — real, dose-dependent bladder scarring that at high

chronic doses can require surgery. A hard medical stop the neurochemical framework
doesn't override: urological symptoms mean escalate to a doctor, not adjust a supplement.

• Daily direction: Foundation methylation + COMT support per the underlying profile; NAC

for glutamatergic/oxidative support and craving reduction. No clean precursor-replacement
move like the stimulant case, because the action is dissociative, not a neurotransmitter
flood.

• The integration point: clinically administered ketamine with integration support is a

genuinely different intervention from recreational use — the library treats the container,
not just the molecule, as the variable.

Worked example C — Kratom (an opioid-type agonist)

Mechanism: 7-hydroxymitragynine is a partial mu-opioid agonist. Withdrawal is opioid-type —
real, physical, 12–24h onset. Often traps people leaning Sage / Depleted Worrier who arrived
via a legitimate opioid or pain problem the system didn't solve.

• Profile that predicts it: COMT Met/Met, often + MTHFR T/T; OPRM1 and CYP2D6 status

change the medication plan.

• Taper/bridge — the key move: buprenorphine manages opioid-type withdrawal, kratom

included, and is requestable by name at an ER or from a MAT prescriber. If OPRM1 is G-

The honest timeline — for a substance-damaged brain (the 'addict virgin'
correction)

Standard recovery timelines were measured on lightly-addicted or non-
addicted people. Your starting point is different, and the guide is explicit
about it: 'exercise improves mood in 2 weeks' becomes 4–8 weeks; '8-week
meditation benefit' becomes 12–16 weeks at a starting dose of 2–5 minutes built up
slowly. The improvement may be happening physiologically before you feel it.

what severe addiction impaired. Exercise-reduces-craving needs a prefrontal cortex
able to act on the reduced signal — so early on it often needs pairing with NAC, the
methylation stack, and (a tier the standard stack omits) nootropic support for
AMPA/BDNF repair. The interventions work. They work more slowly for you. That is
not failure — it's the correct timeline for your actual starting point.

MTHFR (methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase) is the enzyme that converts dietary folate
into 5-methyltetrahydrofolate (5-MTHF) — the active form of folate that the methylation
cycle uses. Methylation is not one biochemical process. It is the backbone of:

methylation-cycle cofactors at rate-limiting steps

•

•

•

consequence of impaired MTHFR, and elevated homocysteine is an independent
cardiovascular risk factor, a neurological toxin (NMDA agonist at high levels), and a
marker of impaired methylation across all the above systems

processes
