## Abstract

This article examines the apophatic writings of Heraclitus of Ephesus. Emphasis is placed on the historical and philosophical analysis of the fragment DK 14 and its interpretations presented in specialized studies. One interpretation reveals a conflation of DK 14 and DK 5. A detailed analysis of the sources presented in this article reveals the erroneousness of this approach and, at the same time, concludes that DK 14 and DK 5, both individually and when considered together, are important for the reconstruction of Heraclitus' apophaticism. The analysis proposes an interpretation of the metaphor "purification by dirt" and its apophatic context is explained. As a result, a proposition is formulated according that DK 14 and DK 5 can be used as a starting point for the historical and philosophical reconstruction of Heraclitus' apophaticism.
