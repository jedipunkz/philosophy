Abstract

This essay investigates the structural isomorphism between The Reality Model (Rijnders, 2025)
— a formal metamodel describing the minimal architecture that any fully intelligible reality must
possess — and the Hebrew Bible and early Christian writings, from Genesis through the Book of
Revelation. The essay proceeds from an explicit methodological commitment that distinguishes
structural isomorphism from metaphorical resemblance: a correspondence is confirmed only
when a biblical element plays the same functional role in the text’s architecture that the
corresponding model element plays in the model’s dependency chain, and when the mapping
operates bidirectionally — the model illuminating aspects of the text that traditional exegesis
could not explain, and the text illuminating aspects of the model that the formal notation left
phenomenologically underdeveloped.

Model’s architecture in precise form, and analyzes the Hebrew Bible’s treatment of the ground,
the field’s intrinsic operation, the character of emergence, and the dynamics of pathology. The

Part Two continues with the healing movement, the New Testament’s structural phenomenology,
