presumably only those societies (tribes) have survived, and thus become
the object of research, which had succeeded in producing, through their
rational and irrational beliefS on the subject of incest, effective in-
hibitions that reduced to a minimum conflicts within the family.

No society hasever succeeded in getting along without the social unit
of the nuclear family (parents and children). Every attrition of that unit
weakens and endangers the whole society. As Murdock emphasizes,
however, there isno more destructive form of conflict than sexual rivalry
and jealousy. The incest taboo alone makes possible the co-operative and
stable family group. Without totally neglecting other causes, Murdock
places the functional theory in the foreground: both the interest ofthe

controls or inhibitions, supported by the strict norms of the culture
