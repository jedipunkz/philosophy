1. Minutes. The minutes of the ninty-ninth meeting were approved as
corrected. (A typographical error was identified in paragraph 5.)

2. Comments and Announcements. The Chairman welcomed three people to their
first COMEX meeting. They are: George Menas, DoD (P), Fred Mannke, Army, and
Dora Brooks who is taking Greg Sieminsk7's place as the Army FSTC
representative. In addition to being Greg's last meeting, it was
last meeting, too.

reported on a meeting he ana nad with
Ruth Greenstein, chair of the COMEX Academic Advisory Panel, concerning future
panel activities. Possible agenda items were discussed for the next meeting
which is scheduled for 8 & 9 May. There is some pressure from the Panel to
hear from a high-level policy maker or two. A short status report on several
issues of interest to the Panel also was discussed. Ruth subsequently wrote
the report to the Panel. (A copy is attached.) Ruth also wrote a letter to
the Assistant to the President for National Security Affairs urging, on behalf
of the Panel, that the National Policy on the Transfer of Scientific and
Technical Information, a draft on which had been reviewed by the Panel in

June 1984, be adopted soon.

3. The DIA SOCRATES Pro ram. | the Program Manager, provided a
detailed description of this oreign availability data base. The four key.
concepts of SOCRATES are:

--Foreign availability is defined as foreign capability plus the
probability of transfer.

--Existing resources of the government are being used to create the
data base.

--Information is identified, collected, assessed, and disseminated by
critical parameters.

--The data base is maintained at a critical element level to support
a wide variety and level of assessment.

then described the 11 products or product matrices, as they are

called, of the data base. Two are called main products, four are called
search mode products, and five, archive mode products. SOCRATES will reach an
initial operational status in about three months.
