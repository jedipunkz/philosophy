INTRODUCTION

This Administrator's Handbook has been prepared to provide a con-
cise overview of Project SOCRATES for any educator who is faced with
the responsibility of developing, implementing, operating, and evaluating
a gifted education program. Although designed for use by an administrator
in either a single school or an entire school district, this information

can also be useful to the teacher of the gifted.

This handbook includes philosophy, general information about Paris

school district, goals, identification procedures, operational procedures,
curriculum, instructional strategies and materials, staff development,
evaluation procedures, public relations and involvement, and replication
provisions~--all of which are unique to Project SOCRATES. Ideally, this
information provides a framework from which administrators and teachers
of other school districts can build a gifted education program which is

in accord with their local philosophy and needs.

Project SOCRATES, funded by Title IV, Part C of the Elementary and
Secondary Education Act, was implemented in August, 1976. Since that
time many good things have been developed and much experience has been
gained. Paris Independent School District is eager to share its program

for the academically gifted with other educators.

STATEMENT OF PHILOSOPHY

In accordance with the United States Office of Education and
the Texas Education Agency, the Paris Independent School District
defines gifted and talented children as being those who have out-
standing abilities and are capable of high performance in any of
the following areas:

1) General intellectual ability

2) Specific academic aptitude

3) Creative or productive thinking
4) Leadership ability

5) Visual and performing arts

6) Psychomotor ability

These children require differentiated educational programs beyond
those normally provided by the regular school program.

The Paris Independent School District's program for the gifted

specifically addresses the following areas: general intellectual

ability, creative or productive thinking, and leadership ability.

This program is designed to parallel Paris Independent School Dis-
trict's philosophy of education which states that all students should
be provided with the opportunity to develop academic potentials and with
the opportunity to use their acquired skills and knowledge to think

logically, critically, and creatively.

SOCRATES is a project for academically gifted students in grades
four and five. For the purposes of this project, an academically gifted
student is described as one who possesses expanded academic capabilities
for problem solving, creative thinking, evaluation, leadership, and the
ability to adjust to change.

Project SOCRATES is designed in consideration of the following
basic assumptions and observable facts:

1) The academically gifted differ from others in learning

capability. They learn faster and remember more and
they tend to think more deeply about what they learn.

2) The academically gifted, as adults, remain similarly
advanced and tend to assume distinctive leadership roles
in whatever lines of activity they engage.

The regular school curriculum only barely approximates
the demands of either the greater learning capacity or
the anticipated leadership roles of the academically
gifted.

The most feasible educational structure for meeting the
needs of the academically gifted is the specialized in-
structional resource setting into which gifted students
are brought together for a portion of the school week for
study and for group and individualized activities.

The academically gifted learn basic language and compu-
tational skills quite well in the regular class setting.
The added emphasis in the resource setting should be upon
abstract reasoning, forecasting, creative thinking,
decision making, and planning.

It is from these assumptions and facts and from other information
gained from research, other school districts, specific developmental
projects, education service centers, and the Texas Education Agency
that the SOCRATES goals evolved. The following broad goals are identi-
fied to accomplish the overall intents of the SOCRATES project:

IDENTIFICATION PROCESS

A total of sixty academically gifted students are identified for
participation in Project SOCRATES at the beginning of each school year.
Thirty students are identified in grade four and thirty students are
identified in grade five. These students represent approximately 7.5 percent
of the district-wide enrollment in grades four and five.

Identification criteria are designed to specify only academically
gifted students. The Texas Education Agency in accord with the United
States Office of Education dgerines gifted and talented youth as those who
are identified by professional educators and who by virtue of outstanding
abilities are capable of high performance in any of the following areas,
singly or in combination: /

1) General intellectual ability

2) Specific academic aptitude

3) Creative or productive thinking
4) Leadership ability

5) Visual and performing arts

6) Psychomotor ability

Identification criteria for Project SOCRATES address only three areas
of giftedness--general intellectual ability, creative or productive think-
ing, .and leadership ability.

Identification criteria are totally objective and consist of only
two standardized testing instruments--the Otis-Lennon Mental Ability Test,
a group intelligence test, and the School and College Ability Tests, a
group aptitude test which measures verbal and mathematical reasoning abilities.

The identification process is accomplished during the first six-
weeks period of the school year. All students in grades four and five in
the school district are administered both testing instruments in order
to give each a chance for selection. Data for each grade level are

rated separately. Students are ranked according to their scores on
each test. Each student's rank position on the Otis-Lennon and his/her
rank position on the SCAT are then averaged to derive the student's

rank assignments. The thirty students having the highest rankings are
identified for participation from each grade level yielding the sixty
project participants.

A student who is a participant in grade four is not automatically
a@ participant in grade five. He/She must undergo the same selection
process as a fifth grade student.

When the sixty project participants are identified, their regular
classroom teachers for both the preceding and the current school year are
consulted for verification of student performance. In case of teacher

disagreement concerning a certain student's performance, the parent(s)

of that student are informed, prior to project enrollment, that there is

some question as to whether or not the child can maintain his/her usual

classroom performance and miss one school day each week as result of

participation in the SOCRATES project. The decision for project parti-
cipation is made by the parent(s). If the decision is made to enroll

the child in the project, his/her regular classroom performance is closely
monitored during the first six-weeks period of project operation. At the
end of this period, the regular classroom teacher(s), principal, and
project staff make a final decision regarding the child's continued
project participation. If the child is removed from the project, the
thirty-first studeat according to average rank assignments for his/her
grade level is enrolled in the project, providing that this change occurs
during the first twelve weeks of project operation. After this time,

no “new” students are enrolled in the project.

Upon completion of the identification process, parents of the sixty

SOCRATES participants are informed and asked to attend project orienta-

tion. A parental consent form must be signed by each child's parent(s)

for project participation.

PROJECT OPERATION

Project SOCRATES is managed by a Project Director, who is Assistant
Superintendent for Curriculum and Instruction in Paris Independent School
District; a Project Coordinator, who is Director of Elementary Education
for the school district; one full-time Project Teacher; and, one full-
time Project Teacher Aide. All of these staff members are actively
involved in implementation of the project.

The first six-weeks period of each school year is devoted to district-
wide testing and identification of project students; parent orientation;
curriculum revision; preparation of teacher-made instructional materials;

selection of commercially prepared instructional materials; and staff

development for the project teacher and aide as well as all elementary

staff members. Identified students are enrolled for instruction at the
beginning of the second six-weeks period for the remainder of the school
year.

Project SOCRATES employs a program prototype whereby Monday through
Thursday of each week, approximately fifteen students per day are "pulled
out" of the regular classroom and transported to the district-wide
instructional center located on the Givens Elementary School campus.
Fourth grade students are scheduled in the center on Monday and Tuesday
and fifth grade students are scheduled on Wednesday and Thursday. Students
arrive at the center by 8:45 a.m. and return to their regular school cam-
puses by 3:00 p.m. Students are responsible for all regular classroom

instruction and assignments which they miss during this one day per week.

Friday of each week is used by the project teacher for planning

purposes and for conducting conferences with parents of project students,

principals, and regular classroom teachers.

CURRICULUM

Curriculum for Project SOCRATES is developed as a means of attain-
ing the five broad goals which are identified to accomplish the overall
purpose of the project. This curriculum focuses on higher level think-
ing skills in the areas of creative thinking, forecasting, reasoning,
decision making, and planning. Each of these areas is addressed by a
broad goal.

According to the State Plan for the Education of the Gifted and
Talented devised by the Texas Education Agency, "the gifted and talented
require differentiated educational programs and/or services beyond those
normally provided by the regular school program." It is for this reason
that the SOCRATES curriculum is based on’ higher level thinking skills
and that it is not based on acceleration of the regular school curriculum.
Skills and/or content of the standard subjects in the regular school
curriculum may be taught indirectly but no conscious effort is made to
do so.

The areas of the curriculum are taught in the sequence in which they
are listed above. No certain amount of time is devoted to each of the
areas. Student needs and interests determine the time factor.

A SOCRATES Curriculum Guide has been prepared by the project staff.
Only four broad goals are listed in this guide. Goal One involves creative
thinking; Goal Two involves forecasting; and, Goal Three involves reasoning.
Goal Four combines decision making and planning. Basic skills involved
in these two areas are taught in sequence, but many of the instructional

activities require skills of both areas.

INSTRUCTIONAL STRATEGIES AND MATERIALS

The individualized approach to learning is employed in Project
SOCRATES. Learning experiences are organized around the individual
needs of academically gifted students. Students work alone, in small
groups, and in one large group at different times in the instructional
center. Tutoring, both teacher-student and student-student is utilized.

Each student has the opportunity to experience a feeling of autonomy,
as he/she plays a part in setting his/her own individual objectives and
selecting activities to be undertaken to reach those objectives. A
variety of materials is accessible to the student for selection or
assignment according to his/her needs and interests. Differentiation
of student activity is evident.

As a very vital part of the individualized approach, the project
teacher maintains full responsibility for teaching. His/Her role includes
analyzing, prescribing, instructing, guiding, leading, motivating, coun-
seling, and/or stimulating. He/She is constantly striving to help
each academically gifted child realize his/her potential ability.

A variety of teaching strategies is used to implement the SOCRATES
curriculum. Included are lecture, brainstorming, simulation games,
exploration, research, inquiry, creative innovation, debate, group dis-
cussion, independent study, and computer assisted instruction.

All instructional activities are designed to reinforce the five
curriculum areas. Instructional materials and equipment include an
on-line computer terminal, teacher-made materials, and commercially
prepared materials and kits which involve student activities based upon

thinking and reasoning skills.

STAFF DEVELOPMENT

Paris Independent School District staff development in gifted edu-
cation involves an awareness level, which is designed for ail elementary
staff members, and a more in-depth training level, which is designed for
the project teacher.

Upon initial implementation of Project SCCKATES, an awareness
session was conducted for all elementary staff members. General informa-
tion about gifted education and an overview of the project were presented.
Regular classroom teachers of students who were identified for project
participation were provided more specific information concerning project
operation. Middle school staff members were not included in this awareness
session due to unlike staff development schedules; however, in the case
of project replication, it is recommended that the middle school staff be
included in order to provide for student follow-up.

In addition to the awareness session, several "update" sessions have
been provided for all elementary staff members. All dissemination materials,
including the project brochure, the curriculum guide, the staff slide/tape
presentation and the student slide/tape presentation have been shown during
these sessions. A detailed explanation of the curriculum guide has been
given to the project participants' regular classroom teacher(s).

Selection of the project teacher and teacher aide was made by the
project director and coordinator. Teacher qualifications included out-
standing past performance as a regular classroom teacher, a high energy
level, flexibility, and openmindedness to new approaches and ideas. Teacher

aide qualifications included ability to perform required instructional

and clerical duties and the ability to "meet the public" graciously as
many on-site visitations by educators, parents, and citizens of the
community were expected.

In-depth training for the project teacher was initiated by providing
time for that person to review available literature on gifted education.
In addition, that person was sent to a national conference on the gifted
and the talented. In the fall of 1976, avenues for staff development
in gifted education were limited.

Presently, the Texas Education Agency has grouped the twenty edu-
cation service centers of Texas into five divisions to form the Texas
Regional Consortia on the Education of the Gifted and Talented. The
principal objective of each consortium is to provide a vehicle for
delivering staff development opportunities on the education of gifted/
talented students to local school personnel. The project teacher as
well as the coordinator are participants in this training.

In the case of project replication, it is recommended that in-depth
training of the project teacher be comprehensive and continuous. Suggestions
for this training include review of past and current literature, attendance
of conferences sponsored by national and state professional organizations,
participation in training sponsored directly by the Texas Education Service
Centers and the Texas Education Agency, participation in conferences
and/or courses of instruction sponsored by institutions of higher learning,

and on-site visitations to gifted education programs.

EVALUATION

Evaluation of student progress in Project SOCRATES is based on
growth in the five curriculum areas. The project teacher holds a minimum
of two conferences each school year with the parent(s) of each SOCRATES
student and with the regular classroom teacher(s) of each SOCRATES student
in order to report individual progress. A file containing examples of
student performance on written activities is maintained for each student
and is used as the basis for coriference reporting. No written grade
report is given. 3

Evaluation of program effectiveness is based on both objective and
subjective data. SOCRATES students are pre- and post-tested to show
gain with locally-developed instruments, which measure student performance
in the five curriculum areas and with the School and College Ability Tests,
which measure verbal and mathematical reasoning abilities. In addition,

@ parent evaluation instrument is completed by each SOCRATES student's
parent(s). Data gathered using these instruments are treated statistically.
Subjective data are gathered from the parent evaluation instrument

in the form of comments, Additional data are obtained from regular
classroom teachers and parents of the SOCRATES students in scheduled con-
ferences. The project staff, principals, regular classroom teachers, and

parents all have input in project evaluation and planning.

Efforts by the project staff to ensure public awareness and
support of Project SOCRATES are directed toward the following groups of
people: the local Board of School Trustees, all Paris Independent
School District staff members at the elementary level, parents of project
participants, and other citizens of the community.

Upon completion of the initial planning and developmert of the
project and prior to its implementation, the Board of School Trustees
was informed of the ces and asked for approval. During the first year

time, the Board has been kept abreast of progress through status reports
made by the project director and coordinator and through prepared dis-
semination materials.

Special efforts are made by the project staff to keep all Paris ISD
elementary staff members informed of the progress being made in SOCRATES.
An awareness session and “update” sessions have been conducted during
which the staff slide/tape presentation and the student slide/tape presenta-
tion have been shown. The project brochure and curriculum guide are
accessible to all staff members.

Regular classroom teachers in grades four and five are directly involved
in SOCRATES, as some of their students are the participants. Arrangements
are made for each teacher to visit the district-wide center in order to
become familiar with the curriculum, instructional strategies, and
instructional materials. Each teacher is provided a personal copy of the

Parents of project students are continuously informed of SOCRATES.

Bach school year, upon completion of the identification process, parent
orientation is conducted. All relevant aspects of the project are explained,
the project brochure is distributed, slide/tape presentation(s) are shown,

A minimum of two conferences with parents of each student are scheduled
during the school year. Pupil progress is reported and suggestions are
made by the project teacher as to how the parent(s) can encourage further
development of student ideas and/or independent studies initiated in the
instructional center.

At the end of the school year, the project staff hosts an open house
for the parents. At this time student products are displayed and/or students
provide a culmination activity which is a result of their combined effort.

Parents are encouraged to give suggestions for project improvement
any time during the school year. A special effort is made to gain their
input during parent conferences. In addition, a survey in which parents
complete an evaluation form is conducted each school year.

REPLICATION

SOCRATES establishes a practical and effective program for aca-
demically gifted students. The project may be replicated in its
entirety or in any of its component parts. Specific components which
may be duplicated are the project brochure, the slide/tape presentation
developed by the project staff, the slide/tape presentation developed
by the project students, the curriculum guide, the administrator's
handbook, and all project operation forms.

Project SOCRATES can be readily replicated at another site. Essential
items include a regular classroom; a teacher who is Flexible, is open to
new ideas, and is eager to become involved in gifted education; testing
materials for identification and evaluation processes; and, recommended
teacher-made and commercially packaged instructional materials.

Excluding the salary of one project teacher, a school district could

sufficiently implement Project SOCRATES for approximately $2,500 as the

first-year cost. This figure represents testing and instructional

materials only. The salary of the teacher aide and the fee for services
for Computer Assisted Instruction are excluded, as these items are com-
pletely optional. If students are to be transported to a district-wide
instructional center, then $750 should be added for transportation, result-
ing in a total first-year implementation cost of $3,250, excluding the
teacher's salary. A budget worksheet which reflects essential and optional

The SOCRATES staff will send printed information upon request and
