Gheorghiu-Dej was born on 8 November 19017 at Barlad, Moldavia, of a
working class family. His father, Tanase Gheorghiu, worked in the local
small industries. The extreme poverty of his parents! home strongly

attitude to the labor problem. He had to go to work at the age of ll.
At 15 he was an Slectrician apprentice with the Steaua-Romana at
Moinesti, Moldavia. His training as an electrician was completed in the
oil industry at Campins.

In 1917 Gheorghiu was a witness to the rebellion of the Russian soldiers
on the Meldavian front. The soldier committees which were established
impressed him strongly. His revolutionary opinions were reinforced by
the frequent strikes at thet time among the workers in the oil industry.
He took an active part therein and distinguished himself as an organizer
of groups to fight the strike-breakers.

After completing his term of military service Gheorghiu worked for the

were led by sensible elements, who. tried to keep the workers out of the
“class war," Gheorghiu, hauwever, proposed leading the workers to better
pay by the revolutionary read. For this he was summarily dismissed,
Shortly thereafter he found employment with the large Gotz & Company saw
works in Galati, and iater he worked again in the street railway shops.

The Fourth Congress of the Rumanian Labor Party, in 1928, proposed to ef—
fect a unio with the Bolshevists, but this was not accomplished because
the later congress of the labor union at Timisoara in 1929 voted to
exclude the Communist syndicate. This left the latter no alternative

but to proceed illegally. The more radical workers in the railway shops
organized an extensive underground activity. Gheorghiu was a member of
the syndical committee, In this capacity he came into closer association
with the Communist party organization and became a Party member. His
task was to organize a party cell in the railway workshops. Later, the
larger responsibility of lecal organizer of the party was transferred to
him. In 1931 he was sent to Bucharest as a delegate to the national
conference of the so-called "Dead Opposition."

In August 1931 Gheorghiu was transferred from the workshops at Galati to
those at Dej as disciplinary punishment for his agitation activities.

This is how he acquired his name-suffix "Dej." At Dej he immediately
organized a local section of the Red AMR (Workers! Aid Organization of the
Labor Union}. At the general meeting of the railway workers of Dej, he
offered a manifesto for a program that accorded with the Communist Party

forwarding the workers! rebellion.

Then came the firsat great mass demonstrations in the railroad shops at

Gheorghiu-Dej was steadily insistent on making the Marx-Lenin doctrine

Petru Groza, the former Prime Minister and present. President of the

orders and carrying out important decisions of the Kremlin.

The danger points in the policy of Gheorghiu-Dej are the agrarian policy,
the carrying through of collectivization, and the problem of Rumanian
goods deliveries to the Soviet Union. Moscow appears to understand

that in Rumania a balance between the production of the country and the

amounts to nothing less than recognition of a declared Rumanian concept
and thereby a Rumanian national brand of Conmunism, For Moscow it is

an experiment; for Gheorghiu-Dej a hazard that may cost him his life.
