It is important to analyze Nasser’s growth sources to understand his ideological values
and approaches to perceive the meaning of Nasser's leadership in Egypt. It should be remembered:

First — he was the first Egyptian leader who ruled Egypt since the Pharaoh time. He
was a Nile native, a true Egyptian.

Third — his leadership was based on a series of political successes — elimination of the

Although in the past this phenomenon was based on land ownership and wealth, in

factors, which were related to the non-institutionalization of Islam, and the absence of class and
ethnic ties.

Political clientelism has united the political system for centuries. It was informal,
personalistic and was based on unifying leadership. The “divide and conquer” tactic was accepted

cooperating between them and being loyal to him. In this sense, the statement that post-coup
Egypt is an “integrative and non-competitive society” is correct. However, the charismatic
phenomenon is a result of severe socio-political crises. The modernization crises are the main
causes of the need for charismatic leadership. Namely, charismatic leadership is a function of
internal situation in developing countries — the desire to achieve consensus, legitimacy and
integration.

The researcher Dekmejian analyses Nasser’s leadership by the charism theory he
believes, and the researcher Weber offers a tri-polar interpretation of historical and social change.
In times of severe socio-political crises and the traditional society falling, the charismatic
personality rises, becomes routine and leads to regime bureaucratization. Without entering into
theoretical limitations, the Egyptian socio-political situation demonstrates the validity of Nasser’s
charismatic personality. It had its own image, communication ability, influence, and credibility —
the three skills that every charismatic personality must have. His leadership was largely
personalistic, and the political system is clientelist. It is essential to largely analyze this subject to

2.2 The influence of external factors on Nasser’s policy

A new era began in the Middle East in general and in relation to the Palestinian
problem in particular following the Kadesh operation results and the Suez Battle in October-
November 1956, Jamal Abdel Nasser was the charismatic leader of Egypt and the entire Arab
world. The first result of his political victory was his massive appeal to the Arab region to establish
his leadership in the Arab world after he was recognized as the undisputed leader of Egypt. Nasser
politically conquest the Arab world through preaching the idea of Arab nationalism (al-Kumiya al-
Arabiya) which was the axis of Arab politics in the reviewed period and after it. This idea was
mentioned in some of his speeches in 1955, but without explaining its meaning. In his speech on
November 7, 1957, Nasser began the long and vigorous campaign for the idea. The period between
1957-1961 was the heyday of Arab nationalism and its peak in the United Arab Republic (UAR)
which was established following the union between Egypt and Syria in February 1958. This
nationalist plan was the essence of Nasserism. An entire generation of Arab leaders was educated
following Nasser’s theory. Nasser preached an active and militant Arab nationalism that would
use all means, including force, to achieve its goals. He believed that in the Arab nationalism he
preaches, he expresses the aspirations of the Arab nation more than any other Arab leader in his
time. He defined several goals for Arab nationalism, including the political, social, and economic
liberation of the Arab world and elimination of imperialism and Arab reaction. Arab nationalism
was designed to fill the empty space created in the Arab world after eliminating the Western
influence. He believed that Arab nationalism is the strongest weapon of the Arab world and the
guarantee for its security. Arab nationalism means Arab solidarity (later he added “Arab
uniqueness”) that is based on independence of Arab countries, later he has added contents as
positive neutrality, non-identification, social justice and real democratic life.5

In one of his speeches, he defined Arab nationalism as a “spiritual movement” and a
“historical movement”, without explaining these terms. He threatened to use military force to
protect Arab nationalism, and indeed invaded Yemen in 1962-1967. Nasser divided the Arab world
into his allies and his enemies, nationalists and imperialism agents, progressives and

3.1 The origin of Abdel Nasser’s Arab socialism lies in three motives

a. Actions taken to ensure the establishment and physical security of the regime
(enemy elimination and the “reaction”), cultivating layers of those who benefit materially, or at
least psychologically, from the regime existence.

b. Steps taken to concentrate the capital assets and human resources required to
fulfill the regime’s ambitions, which are ambitions for prestige, for pretentious industrialization,
for a major military buildup and creating other necessary conditions the regime think necessary
to fulfill its great ambition — hegemony over the Arab world.

c. It should be noted that additional steps with sincere social consciousness
background of the leader and the top brass, who strive for more social justice for the Egyptian
people within the country were taken.°

3.3 The effect of the national coup

“The coup heralded the end of imperialism and the regime liberation from the
domination of feudalism and capitalism, for the establishment of social justice and the elimination
of the contradictions between the classes, and for the rescue of the oppressed from their
oppressors. The coup will turn employees into unexploited property owners and will work for the
benefit of all classes,” in these words defined the President of Egypt, Abdel Nasser, the
fundamental ideological principles of his regime, when it began on July 22, 1961 with the
realization of “Arab socialism” in the “Land of Nile.” Nasser’s “Arab socialism” grew and
developed on the ground of the Egyptian reality and its unique economic-social conditions.
Although ideological foreign currents, some global and some Arab, had an important part in the
social ideological development of the Egyptian coup leaders, the Nasser’s “Arab socialism” is
mainly an Egyptian creation and a result of a pragmatic coping with unique historical conditions
and consequences of international power centers that operated and operate in this reality, and its
development should only be understood against this background.”

4.4 The new class

The social gap widened as the mechanism’s control over the resources of the Egyptian
economy increased, because of the many nationalizations in Egypt, especially since the beginning
of “Arab Socialism” implementation in July 1961. This development even increased the number of
special “white collar” employees in the upper class who have many pleasures and powers. While
the senior manager layer was only 0.1% of the entire government apparatus, the layer of senior
managers in the public sector — the “new class” — was 1.5% of all the employed clerkships. The
“new class” is the social stratum that receives the most benefits and material pleasures under the

6. Conclusion

Economic development was the most important goal in developing countries, after the
struggle for independence, both in terms of the regime’s priority and in terms of the leadership’s
promises. Without promises of improving living standards, achieving industrialization and
modernizing society, there will be considerable difficulties in achieving the regime’s legitimacy.
The process of socio-economic change in most developing countries is inconsistent,
discontinuous, fraught with obstacles and dependent on foreign capital.

The main means of overcoming these crises (as noted in the work describing Nasser’s
work) is the creation of national emotions and symbols to bring about broad community
commitment. Nationalism and comprehensive independence provide an essential psychological
basis for achieving legitimacy and authority for leadership, and these are expressed mainly in their
context with external factors because of the leadership's turn to an active foreign policy.

Most of the goals that Nasserism set for itself were not achieved.

Furthermore, Nasserism failed to fulfill the historical mission it took on. Its failures or
lack of achievements in each of these areas of action ultimately eroded the validity of the vision
and the credibility of the entire movement and were a major factor in the decline of Nasserism.

The regimes that were supposed to strengthen the working class, create equality, and
reduce gaps failed to reach the working class, and the situation of the poor did not improve, and
in fact, the situation of the lower class and the fringe of the middle class did not improve, and even
worsened, when most of the resources were invested in:

A. Securing the physical foundation of the regime.

B. Concentration of capital in the hands of the regime, industrialization of

C. Failure to implement and provide true social justice.

In fact, it was not possible to eliminate the remnants of feudalism and carry out an
agrarian reform and industrial revolution that were the indispensable condition not only for the
possibility of the rise of the working class, but also for the uninterrupted development of the
middle classes. The young and weak working class could not serve as an alternative to carrying out
the “bourgeois-democratic revolution” which subsequently developed into a real socialist
revolution. The Arab countries (Egypt in particular) did not have the ability to provide a solution
to the labor shortages that were constantly expanding because of natural increase and the
migration of the unemployed from the countryside to the city. Most of the proletariat lacked
elementary education and literacy and were captive to foreign, religious, nationalist class
ideologies. It was difficult to influence and reach the lower strata of the middle class, which is a
large one.

and its weight in the general population and even among the urban population. In Egypt, the most
developed of the Arab countries, the percentage of industrial workers did not reach 10% of the
total urban population in 1958 (i.e. 6 years after the officers’ coup)!

But we should not underestimate the power of the Arab Socialist Union (ASU) that
was established (the Arab Socialist Union) and its influence (Nasser's vision of pan-Arabism). The

who was the Secretary-General of the ASU in the two years preceding the war, managed to
transform it from a bureaucratic organization lacking political influence into an organization with
political and organizational capacity whose political influence was considerable. For example,
when Nasser resigned in June 1967, Sabri used his power and influence on the Socialist Youth
Organization to prevent Nasser's resignation and the transfer of the presidency to Zakaria Muhi
al-Din. It was the members of the Socialist Youth Organization who launched mass
demonstrations on June 9 and 10 that returned Nasser to power.
