1973

Beirut — Lebanon

munist Manifesto. It was prepared by the Cultural Bureau

The historical mission of the working class is defined
by the radical liberation of humanity from the yoke of
imperialism and all forms of slavery arising in the course
of class struggle in all communities of the world; it is also
defined by the relentless struggle for the realization of class-

less society as a historical objective in accordance with
which various links are determined for the political and
class struggle of peoples and nations the world over.

The working class concept, which is the most radical
concept, is responsible for the comprehension and assimi-

lation fall phenomena and changes occuring in the pro-
cession of international revolution, so as to be capable of
bringing about uniform revolutionary replies within the
context of the great socialist movement.

Hence, the comprehensiveness of national and social
struggle of the peoples all over the world, while approach-

ing the exclusiveness or workers’ struggle in the
world, is demanded to assimilate the conditions and char-
acteristics of the struggle of each nation and people sep-
arately, so as to specify the strategy of world workers’

fight in line with the conceptions of international revolu-
tion and for the common interests of hard toilers the

world over. This assumes, under various conditions, that
the consideration of any political and class question any-
where in the world should proceed from the workers’ re-
volutionary standpoint and stem from the theoretical start-
ing point of socialist revolution.

The diversification and complexity of peoples’ is-
sues are in need of this most radical concept which is
capable of unifying such issues; and without this most radi-
cal concept, viz: the workers, it will be impossible to
unify peoples’ issues in a developed militant manner.

A superficial, or confirmist, concept can provide a
stumbling and precarious combination of human struggle
issues, which may achieve simple and narrow coordination
and collaboration but such consequences are far from of-
fering any methodological course of common struggle per-
fected by the strategy of contemporary international revo-
lution.

The international life of the working class has pro-
vided, through irrefutable and radical experiences,
evidence of the fact that the working class itself has been
capable of steering the variables of human struggle and
transforming them into constant socialist transformation
principles which have imposed the socialist reconstruction
in vast areas of the world.

Following the glorious Soviet October Revolution, the
positions of socialist struggle have been consolidated and
basic opportunities provided for the liberation and social
progress of peoples, within the framework of the ever-

8

growing prestige and historical role of the working class
in the orientation of social struggle and liberation.

As the Soviet October Revolution represented the
great starting point of the international revolutionary
movement, the assimilation of the process and comprehen-
sion of its specific conditions makes it basically inevitable
to devote serious attention to two points -

1. Intensive deduction of revolutionary lessons and ex-
periences introduced by the October Revolution, close
maintenance of revolutionary inspiration and an ever-
vital revision of the October Revolution.

2. Comprehension of the revolutionary characteristics of
the world revolutionary process and scientific elabo-
ration of the consistences of national class struggle,
in such a manner as to reach for the programmes of
revolutionary struggle in various countries and

forge a profound linkage between such programmes,
from the angle of the more radical worker concept,
with a view to incorporating such subsistences into
the revolutionary formulation of class struggle at an
international level, ie. the struggle between «social-

ism» and «capitalism», as the diversified, complex
and versatile forms of struggle in various countries
of the world are in need of merger into the funda-
mental class struggle currently dominating the world.

As the contemporary revolutionary process is respon-
sible — in all cases — for the merger and polarization of

political and class struggles, into a common international

2

level specifically by the revolutionary confrontation of
capitalism, the recognition of the main characteristics and
conditions in any political and class struggle, and in any

national or nationalist militant movement, it is the funda-
mental and constant point through which the world re-
volutionary process can be elaborated.

As the October Revolution has smashed the world
capitalist siege by liberating one sixth of the world, it has
opened wide the horizons against the isolation imposed
on revolutionary forces.

With the transformation of socialism into a colossal
camp-shaped reality, the revolutionary isolationalism which
may cover itself by formative, simple or very limited ad-
justments, should end categorically after such transforma-
tion, hence the term «world revolutionary process» which
is an ascription of a number of national and nationalist

revolutionary processes and class movements lacking in
sound unity or wanting in proper unitary strategy confin-
ing it to the concept of «world revolutionary process».

Actually, there can be no other alternative than ad-
mitting the presence of numerous revolutionary processes,
for such recognition of numerousness and versatility is the
starting point towards the union or unity of such revolu-

tionary processes which are merged into the «world rev-
olutionary process». The working class — the decisive
force in all human societies and the basic human entity
which realizes the prerequisites of progress forwards and
upwards — is alone capable of achieving the merger of
the set of national and nationalist revolutionary processes

10

within the procession of world socialist struggle, and into
the more realistic personification of the «world revolution-
ary process».

Now, how does this take place? That is the question!

Of course, there are certain criteria and
instant replies, however, they are not only means _ of

changing the current status quo, for part of it is physical-
ly built into them.

In the realities of current struggle between «socialism»
and «capitalism», there are reciprocal «swap» operations,
as socialism borrows certain capitalist techniques and vice

versa. Other interactions also interfere in determining the
course of struggle and the degrees of its acuteness and re-
laxation. Therefore, the constant and regular recourse to
the study of the historic role of the working class in the

objective outlet from the tumult of indigenous additions
arising in the course of multi-revolutionary processes
which are the outcome of struggle isolation whose «nega-

tion» is counteracted by simple and meagre international
coordination.

No doubt, the consolidation of worker concept and
promotion of the direct effectiveness of the working class,
together with the alignment of national struggle program-
mes with the working class positions and enrichment of
the pioneering role of the working class at the international
level, without accepting its fragmentation into equal posi-
tions in which the effective pioneering role of workers is
only highlighted at the local level, are basic arguments for

11

the strategy of international revolution, or the « world re-
volutionary process ».

Without reference to the opinions opposing the his-
torical mission of the working class, which may generally

deny the capability of the working class to undertake such
a historical mission, or even deny the presence of such a
mission, intensive consideration should be devoted to the
means through which the working class can carry out its
historical mission, without impairing the conviction of
large numbers of the people of its relevance and historical
origins.

Obviously, there are several matters which may lend
evidence to considerable aspects of the reply sought.

There is the duty of the working class movement,
at the international level, towards promoting its revolution-
ary effectiveness with a view to attain the progressive and

revolutionary tasks, by reducing the degree of guardian-
ship being practised by several political parties and govern-
ments thereupon.

In fact, guardianship may take place as a result of
shortage in the working class qualifications, both in terms
of «theory» and «cadre», but it may be an outcome of
exploiting this indigenous factor to impose guardianship
that will hinder patching this gap up; therefore, it can in
no way be consistent with the character of the historical
mission.

Being an emergency operation, it should render ser-
vice to the revolutionary process related to highlighting

12

the real capability of the working class which, if properly
resurrected and mobilized, would nullify every emergency
and temporary practice, however inevitable it might be.

The class revolutionary experiences which embody a
considerable proportion of originality, are the framework
within which the working class operates independently so
as to permit the development of the revolutionary dimen-
sions of its pioneering role: such experiences provide the
working class with various factors required for movement
towards independence-oriented leftist practices. In the coun-
tries of the «third world» where petit bourgeois parties
play a significant role in the attempt to contain the work-
ing class potentialities so as to deviate them — through
speaking on its behalf, hence the guardianship; this is evi-
dent in the slogans and practices of reformist socialist par-
ties which maintain bright «selective» principles — from
embarking upon techniques and practices adopted in the
field of reality. The theoretical, political and practical re-
evaluation of the working class role is a vital necessity.
This is more so in view of the fact that the October Revo-
lution and subsequent revolutionary developments have

revealed the feasibility of a socialist revolution in an

under-developed country, which also implies the feasible
emergence of an effective role for the working class.

In his message to the American workers on August
20, 1918, Lenin wrote

«We realize that conditions have driven our Russian
platoon to the forefront of the socialist proletariat; this is

13

not attributable to our merits but to the exceptional under-
development of Russia.»

This vividly and directly explains the possibility of
acquiring a radical outcome from a backward situation.
The actual exceptional under-development of certain
countries within the areas termed as «the third world» are
qualified, under current conditions, to provide highly
effective workers’ militant situations capable of reducing
the gap between them and the developed countries by
means of the same struggle.

A basic topic which can be tackled immediately after
this review, namely the consolidation of the «world con-
cept» which involves the condition of most radical implica-
tion, 1.e., a concept of historical implication. The working
class concept reaches out to any issue anywhere in the
world, for comprehension, analysis and scrutiny.

As the world is forging amazingly rapid links, the co-
lossal developments of Asia, Africa and Latin America,
which are, in the ultimate analysis, important class deve-
lopments, require a common working class comprehen-
sion which will natuarlly rise to the level of practice.

The worker movement in any European country can
in no way ignore or overlook the developments of Arab
«Palestine», «Bengal», «Iran», etc. The tempestuous con-
flicts of Asia, Africa and Latin America should not be
approached as secondary events.

The international development of workers’ struggle

14

should always keep Lenin’s words fresh in our memory,

as confirmed by the pioneering Soviet October Revolution,
that «pioneering tasks of the international revolution may
be commenced in under-developed, nay extremely
under-developed, countries».

When the battlefields of Asia, Africa and Latin Ameri-
ca are overwhelmed by ferocious and «tragic» conflicts,
the obligation of the working class the world over, and in
the developed countries in particular, is defined in the man-
ner of highlighting the class nature of such conflicts, and

supporting the struggle of their peoples for national self-
determination exclusively free from the domination of
world imperialism.

Therefore, it is imperative that a vivid international
public opinion be formed towards any political, social or
national issue the world over, regardless of the spatial
location of such issue and its relationship to the «limelight»
on the international stage and its commercial communica-
tions media.

The workers’ opinion should maintain the conditions
and supercede the characteristics of any status quo. So long
as «capitalism» is bound to die under the impact of the
socialist struggle of the working class and peoples of the
world, the «socialism» which is established in a number of
countries, and as postulated, by «scientific socialism» on
the decadence of the state in a classless society, is not the
final form of human life.

Accordingly, the workers’ concept is provided with

15

a wide arena of movement and independent radical initia-
tive, to resurrect the «current revolutionary process» which

has lost the lustiness of the three «internationals».

To provide a formation for the «world revolutionary
process» emphasis should be made on:

a) A continuous social revolution in the West.

b) Leftist national revolution in the so called «third
world.»

c) Democratic settlements to strengthen the com-
munity links of the socialist countries.

THE WEST AND SOCIAL REVOLUTION

In 1919, Lloyd George, Britain’s Prime Minister told
a group of British workers, «Strike implies a challenge to
the Government, and its success will give rise to a serious
constitutional crisis. If within the state a challenging force
should emerge, such force should, then, be prepared to
take over the Government assignment, and if not it should
withdraw and accept the state authority».

Eyer since that date, the British working class has only
satisfied the «conditional answer». So has the working
class in the U.S., West Germany and other European
countries.

So long as the topic of «world revolutionary process»
comes atop all theoretical and political discussions, the

16

world working class role in guiding this revolutionary pro-
cess requires direct and effective participation on the part

of workers in the developed countries in the international
revolutionary movement. Hence emerges the following
irony as evident in two facts.

J. ‘The imperialist West is the No. 1 enemy of the
peoples of the world, and is the classical embo-
diment of capitalist exploitation and class strug-
gle.

2. The pioneering role of the working class in the
world revolutionary process assumes that work-
er’s liberation is the deliverance of the broadest
masses.

The world-wide generalization of this pioneering role
renders workers’ struggle a direct contribution to the
peoples’ liberation movement, which places on the should-
ers of workers in the imperialist west a basic and direct
responsibility in supporting peoples’ struggles for liberation
and social progress.

However, has the second fact managed to occupy its
realistic place in confronting the imperialist West? Has the
working class in the imperialist West managed to sponsor
the tasks of the world revolutionary process, as it holds
advanced social and civilizational positions at the interna-
tional level?

Here lies the irony !

A lot of evidence indicates that a far-reaching
civilizational acclimatization is being conducted on the

17

working class in the U.S. and imperialist West, which is
directly affecting the indicators of world workers’ struggle

and the inevitability of the workers’ pioneering role in hu-
man struggle as whole.

Many developing nations have taken the initiative to
raise the banner which should have been raised by the

worker movement! a number of militant movements, e.g.
the «Black» movement in the U.S. and certain other move-
ments in Europe are undertaking the «struggle» assign-
ments of the workers within the context of socialist pers-
pective, whereas the workers’ movement in the West has
fallen short even of the classical practices of the 1840's.

The decisive consequence of developments in the im-
perialist West has been the consideration of any social re-
volution as a «socialist revolution».

In other words, the imperialist West which the great
veterans of socialism had considered the prospective opti-
mal battlefield of class struggle and world socialist revo-
lution is witness of the working class abandonment of

socialist revolution, thus degenerating the concept of social
revolution and declining to pose any threat to imperialist
and capitalist interests in general.

As the consequences of the failure on the part of the
working class in the imperialist West to assimilate the pre-
requisities of the world revolutionary process the coloniz-
ed and semi-colonized nations suffer a lot of sacrifices
and difficulties. The urgent question to be seriously ex-
amined is «how can the working class in the imperialist

18

west carry out the basic tasks in the world revolutionary
process and what can it offer the colonized under-de-
veloped and developing nations? »

The revolutionary change which can transform the
imperialist West into a socialist West may be horrible, but
it will undoubtedly involve providing the key to the ques-
tion of national and nationalist liberation and struggle in
Asia, Africa and Latin America.

The working class in the imperialist West is well
aware of the dimensions of the said question as a whole.
Its indulgence in the unilateral process of capitalist civili-
zational construction, and its acclimatization under the
conditions of capitalist institutions may expel it from the

world revolutionary process or keep it crippled and passive
within it. Obviously the story of the «current world eco-
nomic market» has made a large proportion of workers in
the imperialist West afford the «costs» of their dual pro-
ducer-consumer position, within a balance protected by
capitalist laws.

While many spots in the world are boiling up with
fierce class uprisings and wars, the «world economic
market» hides out behind the curtain of the parliamentary
outlook to deal with problems of class struggle. While the

Vietnamese fighter, the Laotian partisan and Arab Pales-
tinian Commando carry arms to defend the homeland and
freedom, the worker in the imperialist West has killed no
one in support of the world revolutionary issues, and the
placard he raises in protest against the governments of
the imperialist West is not the bullet shot by the worker
of Paris commune.

19

In fact, this rematk can be cross-argued in terms of
the wide-spread discrediting of the inevitability of resort

to «revolutionary violance» techniques in the internal polit-
ical and social conflicts; it is in no way permissible that
things be left to an assumed parliamentary fate which

might admit the inevitability of the peaceful revolutionary
triumph.

Among the revolutionary process conditions are con-
sciousness, control and orientation of struggle in accord-
ance with the prerequisites of specific revolutionary objec-

tives. Any short-coming in this connection will inevitably
benefit the counter-revolutionary movement, directly or
indirectly.

With the discovery of laws reverting to the inevitable
decadence of capitalism, the study of general crises in the
capitalist system acquires an exceptional significance to
the working class, in view of the fact that such study

provides the working class struggle a realistic capability
to exploit the capitalist system’s crises and draw up pro-
grammes for the socialist struggle which aims at under-
mining the capitalist system as a whole.

While the peoples of Asia, Africa and Latin America
deal joint revolutionary blows to world imperialism, with
the economic, political, military and civilizational crises
of the imperialist West being aggravated the working class
has not offered a fighting experience in demolishing the
summit and structure of the imperialist West, and has
failed to back up the peoples’ struggle for liberation and
social progress.

20

Based on the familiarities of international revolution-
ary process, the link between political and class struggle

of workers has acquired an international dimension. In
other words, any working class demand, be it professional
or otherwise, in any European country, should necessarily

be connected with a national and progressive nationalist
political issue in an Asian country, for instance.

In fact, this linkage is affected by a passive considera-
tion represented in the fact that the heated revolutionary
concepts and practices of the 19th century, and the second

half of the 18th century, were far stronger than they are
now, and the world better integrated. In other words, the
internationalism of the world today has not, at the current
level. offered a realistic concept of the world revolutionary

process, so as to be termed international without an inter-
national revolution in terms of a programme that would
combine various revolutionary movements.

The world revolution is a realistic fact and inevitable
entity confirmed by the innumerable uprisings, revolutions
and rebellions.

However, the world revolutionary process, which
constitutes a fundamental axis in the subject of our dis-
cussion implies a systematic definition for the unity of re-
volutions the world over into the context of a continuous
world revolutionary process.

Should not the working class, before any other entity,
be aware of the significance of such unity between revolu-

tionary movements in the world and all progressive move-

21

ments affiliated with it? It is of course this class, before

any other entity, which is responsible for introducing
pioneering practices in its favour.

Intuitively enough, the working class cannot under-

take this pioneering role without a political party, guided
by the international revolutionary scientific theory, mea-

sured in accordance with the criteria of world revolution

and bound by a revolutionary plan which provides wider
independence to the working class.

It is in favour of the capitalist system which attempts
to tame and acclimatise the working class to prevent the
formation of such a militant political party which is bound

to constitute a class pioneering political force; if, how-

ever, such party should ever exist, the capitalist system
would endeavour to transform it into a labour syndicate

or highlight the priority of professional trade unions in
terms of practice and emergency considerations.

In fact, such comparison between the political party
and syndicate renews the argument on a non-formal and
non-general issue.

The concept of the trade union is embodied in the pre-
paredness of the working class in Britain, the US., or
elsewhere, to stage a labour strike that would paralyse
public life in any of them for the attainment of specific
professional objectives, while such a consideration is not
evident in favour of any Asian or African nation in its
just struggle against imperalists except in a united case.

22

Therefore, the concept of trade untion priority im-
plies that the working class in the imperialist West has
generally waived, in one way or another, certain contents
of the political line of the world revolutionary process, or
has conceived this world revolutionary process within a
less serious context.

Almost no one can ever perceive how the imperialist
West exports its imperialism, and affiliated trends repre-

sented in Zionism and racism, with such a ferocious bar-
barism as has rendered it enemy number one to all peoples
of the world, while from within it has forged an under-

standing with the working class, i.e. the working class it-
self has been conforming to the general conditions of
the imperialist West’s mechanism, thus becoming totally

paralysed in terms of exercising any specific revolutionary
action.

It isimperative to emphasize that the working class
in the U.S., Britain and several European countries actu-
ally undertakes extensive internal and external responsi-
bilities within which its interests are united with those of

the oppressed nations; however, according to the concepts
of world revolutionary process, such working class is not
in a position to undertake these responsibilities in an appre-
ciable positiveness, i.e. through the introduction of the so-

cial revolutionary programme guided by the working class
under conditions imposed by the imperialist West, and thus
shatter the parliamentary myth which has degenerated the
concepts of social revolution and deported the dimensions
of class struggle off the transitional basic objectives which
should culminate such action.

23

The concept of social revolution, which is inevitably
socialist revolution, does not only constitute a revival and
resurrection of the prolitarian spirit of struggle in the im-
perialist West, but also represents the sole response to the
aspirations of oppressed nations within the revolutionary
spirit they create.

The working class in the imperialist West should either
acclimatise itself within the classical imperialist system and

abandon the world revolutionary process, or merge itself
with the revolutionary and liberation movements the world
over, within the role it can efficiently undertake and thus
assume in a pioneering manner; there is no other alter-
native.

THE «THIRD WORLD» AND NATIONAL REVOLU-
TION :

To begin with, reference should be made to the fact
that the East is a permissive expression referring to the

peoples of Asia, Africa and Latin America, to qualify the
ferocious national revolutionary struggle launched by the
oppressed masses against world imperialism and capitalism.

The term of national revolution implies the following

basic concepts :

a) Struggle against imperialism and neo-colonialism
b) Struggle against foreign occupation.

c) Unification of the masses belonging to one nation.

24

d) Unification of the territory belonging to one

nation.

A glance at these concepts would reveal a fact related
to the possibility of their orientation within a bourgeois
democratic channel or a socialist development. This
implies that the polarisation of class struggle and _ the
significant role of the working class within its context are
the two basic guarantees to define the socialist course of
national revolution.

For several considerations, it can be argued that on
certain occasions an improper divergence took place
between the world workers’ struggle and national strug-
gle in the colonised, occupied and oppressed territories,
which resulted in popularising an experience within most
national movements through which the middle and

petit-bourgeois leadership managed to leave _ their im-
prints.

Should the common intuitive concept in the theo-

retical and political analysis of scientific socialism indi-
cate that any national and nationalist struggle, after the
October Revolution, joins the great human effort in re-
sisting capitalism and its vices, then ascribing the char-
acteristic of planning practices to such a postulate is indis-
pensible. National revolutions have not been conceived
properly in the light of classical analyses and attitudes

of workers movements thus this misunderstanding prac-
tically met with the trends of middle-bourgeois and re-

formist national movements without doctrinal justifica-
tion.

25

In accordance with the revolutionary consequences
arising in the course of third world experiences, it

has become imperative that the traditional European out-
look which considers national revolutions as bourgeois or
democratic-bourgeois movements should be revised.

It is also possible, prior to issuing any terminology
such as the non-capitalist course or non-alignment that

heed be taken of the circumstances under which the
movement itself has developed to specify the conflict lines

within the social movement, both implict and explicit.

National revolutions are in urgent need of direct

support by all segments of world revolutionary process,
to provide it with such a material force as would make

up for the shortage in the prerequisites of transition from
the pre-capitalist stage to the socialist stage.

No doubt the socialist countries’ support to nation-
al revolutions and national liberation movements in the
countries of Asia, Africa and Latin America has provided

abundant material potentialities; as Marx pointed out,
the history of the Orient is basically the history of religions.
To this the word «nations» may well be added.

The forces of world imperialism have endeavoured
to destroy the nations of the East, humiliate their masses,

plunder their material resources and deny them the op-
portunity of liberty and progress. In this, the imperialist

forces relied on the reactionary forces of the oppressed
nations to draw up long-range exploitation schemes.

26

In the wake of the compound, long-range programme
for the exploitation, colonisation and impoverishment of
the oppressed nations, class struggle was growingly in-
corporated into national conflicts.

For instance, the Arab nation, with a population of
100 million, has been suffering for more than three cen-
turies under the yoke of imperialism and foreign occupa-

tion, subject to contrasted domination of the Ottomans
and local feudalists, to be followed by British and French
imperialism, Zionism and the big bourgeoisie.

Now this contractual plunder is shared by the U.S.
imperialists, Zionists, Arab royal regimes, the reactionary
monarchy in Iran and all other pockets of neo-colonialism
which have collaborated to dedicate the occupation of
Arab Palestine, other Arab territories and the Arab Gulf.
the plunder of Arab oil and conversion of Arab territory
into military bases.

Hence, it is obvious that the struggle waged by the
masses of the Arab nation is ironically both complex and
simple; it combines several forms of class and
national struggle, which are vividly clear to the Arab
masses which consider Arab unity a central slogan to
maintain their historical entity as a nation and safeguard
their Arab homeland against occupation.

The most ferocious imperialist and Zionist techni-
ques which aim to subdue the Arab masses, plunder their
resources and impoverish their forthcoming generations
is that of occupying the Arab territories, deporting Arab
natives and coercively bringing in alien settlers.

Zi

Hand in hand with this technique goes another im-
perialist technique represented in aggravating disruption.
fragmentation and class conflicts. This, on the other side

of the coin, gives rise to the question of objective techni-
ques of counter attack.

The socialist struggle is basically set against exploi-
tation which renders the worker subject to captivity

under the yoke of the huge capitalist machine. Liberty
is a form of fight against exploitation represented in the
fetters of slavery, and unitary struggle is set against
usurpation emanating from disruption and colonial in-
vasion.

Today, the Arab nation lives under the conditions
of collective exploitation which imperialism, Zionism and

reaction are trying to impose with utmost force. National
struggle is the only means of retaliation, ie. by the Arab.
revolutionary and unitary struggle through which sub-
sistances of national liberation are created.

A hard toiling popular force is capable of leading

the Arab united revolutionary struggle, and any lack in
the resoluteness or determination of such leadership will
undoubtedly drop its hold into the hands of the national
and petite-bourgoisie.

The fundamental consideration in the experience of
the Arab national revolution is represented in the organic

integrity between the nation, the class and the revolu-
tionary party.

28

The Arab nation is a unit in terms of its resolute
revolutionary struggle for the. attainment of liberty, in-

dependence and progress.

It may be mentioned here that the word «struggle» is
relevant. It is necessary in every definition of «nation»
and «class» that they should not be confined to social and

economic concepts, for the class is a unit in terms of its
