FOREWORD
Booker Ngesa Omole

Communist Party of Kenya, Party School

This book denotes the foundational stone of the Communist
Party of Kenya. It is a collection of the Party's communications,
reflections, analyses and debates published in unedited, unhidden
and unaltered form. The articles were written or presented by
members of the Party and many comrades who stand in solidarity
with the socialist movement in Kenya. It sets the ground for
things to come and will prove a helpful resource to future
socialists and revolutionaries who will join the communist
movement in Kenya and around the world.

In sharing these articles, we are placing a point of reference
that will resonate beyond this moment. By presenting it
unsaddled by recriminations or apologies, the raw articles expose
the internal and external struggles of the Party; inviting you to
study, critique and potentially draw inspiration from our Journey.
It is also an intimate fireside view of the Leftist struggle in a pro-
capitalist country devoted to subverting the socialist movement.
We hope you will return time and time again to these articles in
order to collaborate, debate or rebut your own experiences.

While the articles will speak for themselves, we hope they
leave you with four treasured gems: understanding, appreciation,
advancement and authority of the nature and purpose of the
Communist Party in Kenya. With these gems in your pocket, you
will be all caught up and hopefully on the same page as we take
the next steps together.

The understanding that revolution is not a single moment or
event. By going through these articles one by one, you will come
across the parched thoughts, words and disjointed movements
that have helped shape the idea, organisation and movement that

is the Communist Party of Kenya. By going through our history,
the internal decisions and activities that took us from the Social
Democratic Party to the Communist Party of Kenya, you will feel
like you are shedding an old skin of long-held ideas that itched to
come out and contradict the artificial world that has been created
by Capitalist propaganda and propped up by successive pro-
capitalist regimes that have governed Kenya.

An appreciation of the universal nature of the struggle: not
just an internal struggle of the Party or the things happening in
this country. You will be taken outside Kenya. There, you will
hear the same words and read the same songs: these nagging
ideas and itchy thoughts that many Kenyans are experiencing
have a common source. That they are caused by systemic
exploitation and supervised suppression, and Kenya's case is
neither new nor unique. That the same exploitation is taking place
all over the world to the majority of the working class and the
people who drive and sustain their countries, and it is happening
right now. We hope you will find sympathy, but also some relief,
and that, by their struggle, you will also feel seen and united
because you share the same fears, hopes and desires for a better
world—a classless society.

The advancement of Socialism is an experiment that
scientifically applies socialist ideology in the political, economic
and all other aspects of society. It is neither conjecture nor is it
the reactionary rhetoric so favoured by our local leaders. From
these articles, you will learn that Socialism is not a new idea.
Socialism, the scientific doctrine of the liberation of the majority
against the dictatorship of the minority, is on the rise. Fresh from
your appreciation of capitalism's cruel and brutal nature in those
countries, you will cleanse your palette with a collage of articles,
biographies, reviews and studies of the way comrades in other

The authority of the Communist Party of Kenya is

PART I
THE UNDERSTANDING OF
THE COMMUNIST PARTY OF KENYA

An open invitation into the inner workings of the Communist
Party of Kenya, CPK. Before we were CPK, we existed as the
Social Democratic Party of Kenya, or SDP. Like all communists,
we have had to fight for our right to exist within Kenya's
bourgeois legality. This battle is proof that despite the
constitutional provision for Kenyans to form a party of their
choosing—ideology notwithstanding—the capitalist forces are
so strong that the idea was rejected by the political elite and the
state institutions mandated by the same constitution to be
impartial.

In this section, we share our experiences and reveal our
Party's documents, programs and reflections during different
activities, experiences and crisis. We have used all these
struggles and looked back at every crisis as an opportunity to
learn. By learning, we have improved day by day. We will keep
improving to build a strong movement and stable Party based on
rigorous discourse that aligns with Marxist-Leninist philosophy
and espouses the socialist ideas flavoured in our Kenyan society
and African roots.

One interesting thing about this section is the Party's internal
struggles. Some are within the Party itself, for example, 2022
split where a minority broke off to join the Kenya Kwanza
Alliance, and others are internal struggles where revolutionaries
and members share their experiences. It is exhausting; it is a
genuine struggle, and the commitment to the struggle, even for a
short while, changes you as much as it changes others around
you.

THE ARREST AND IMPRISONMENT OF
COMRADE GACHEKE GACHIHI AND THE
SEVEN PEACEFUL DEMONSTRATORS IS A
TRAVESTY OF JUSTICE

Benedict Wachira, Secretary General - SDP

19 December 2014

On 18 December 2014, Kenyans from different mass
organisations held a peaceful demonstration in Nairobi,
protesting the passing of the Security Amendment bill 2014 by
the National Assembly. Their intention was to present a petition
to Parliament, outlining their displeasure with the sections of the
Bill that were in complete violation of Chapter Four (Bill of
Rights) of the Constitution of Kenya.

No sooner had the demonstrators arrived on Parliament road,
than the heavily armed GSU and AP policemen (who were
outside Parliament in their hundreds) descended on them with
Rungus and kicks.

The Policemen, on the orders of OCPD Central, started
arresting some of the demonstrators, all of whom were
demanding to know why they were being arrested.

The eight Comrades from Bunge La Mwananchi Social
Movement; Comrades Kenneth Kirimi, Okello Odhiambo, John
Koome, Wilfred Olal, Francis Sakwa, Dennis Okoth, Dennis
Olema and Gacheke Gachihi (who is SDP’s Secretary for Mass
Mobilisation) were beaten and tortured on their way to Central
police Station, where they spent the night after been denied Cash
bail, and Medical Attention.

They were arraigned at the Milimani Law Courts the
following day (19 December 2014) before Resident Magistrate
Miriam Mugure, where they were charged with participating in
an unlawful demonstration.

The Arrest and Imprisonment of Comrade Gacheke Gachihi and the Seven
Peaceful Demonstrators is a Travesty of Justice

This charge is in itself non-existent, since Article 37 of the
Constitution gives every Kenyan the right to “Peaceably and
unarmed, demonstrate, picket and present petitions to State
officials.”

They were also charged with the “crime” of stating that
“polisi ni wale wale.”

Their lawyers (from KHRC and other organisations) sought
for the dismissal of the charges on the basis that they were
unconstitutional, but the magistrate upheld the charges. The
lawyers then pleaded for cash bail for the eight, but they were
instead slapped with bonds of ksh300, 000 each!

This is not only a travesty of justice, but also a reminder of
the Nyayo era tactics, where the state would use elements in the
Judiciary to curtail and infringe on the rights of Kenyans.

This not only shows how rotten the Judiciary is, in spite of
the reforms being implemented, but also shows how the State is
hell bent to return Kenya to the dark days.

The SDP has instructed its lawyers to petition the Judiciary
Service Commission (JSC) for the removal of Magistrate Miriam
Mugure, on grounds of incompetence.

The SDP is also asking its members and supporters to show
solidarity by attending the mention of the matter on Tuesday 23
December 2014 at Milimani Law Courts, starting 9:00am, where
an application for a free bail/cash bail for all the accused will be
made.

The SDP reminds the Jubilee government, and their elements
in the Judiciary, that the people of Kenya rejected dictatorship
long ago, and we are not going to allow them to drag us back into
repression.

REQUEST FOR INTERNATIONAL

SOLIDARITY
Benedict Wachira, Secretary General - CPK
1 May 2019

Dear Comrades,

The Communist Party of Kenya (CPK) came into being on
the Sth of January 2019, after the Social Democratic Party of
Kenya (SDP) resolved in its 5th Party congress to change its
name. The Social Democratic Party of Kenya, in spite of its
name, has always been a Communist Party with regards to its
ideology, constitution, character and methods of operation.

However, it had maintained this name because of the
dictatorial political environment that historically existed in
Kenya and was politically defeated in 2003, and legally removed
from reality in 2010 with the promulgation of a highly
progressive National Constitution (the Constitution itself was a
result of protracted struggles for multi-party democracy, political
rights, social justice, human rights, and the struggle for a better
future for the Kenyan people of which the Party was instrumental
in formulating).

It is with regard to this new environment and the growth of
the Party that the Party resolved to change its name from Social
Democratic Party of Kenya to the Communist Party of Kenya.

However, the Government of Kenya has refused to
gazette/register the new name, claiming that Kenya is a neo-
liberal capitalist State, and it therefore cannot allow
socialist/communist parties.

This type of thinking is some hang-over from the dictatorship
that was politically defeated in 2003. This decision by the
Registrar of Political Parties has no legal basis whatsoever, and
the Party has vowed to fight for its name and its ideology.

Request for International Solidarity

The Party has, since Monday 4th of February 2019 received
overwhelming support from Kenyans many of whom do not
subscribe to Socialist ideas, but are nevertheless in support of our
constitutional right to follow the political beliefs of our choice.

The Party has planned a series of legal and political actions
to counter such reactionary ideas.

It is in this regard that we also request for solidarity from
revolutionary parties and organizations across the world since
anti-communism in Kenya is anti-communism across the planet.

Long live the Struggle for Socialism!
Long live the Communist Party of Kenya!
Down with Capitalism! Down!

II. Capitalism and imperialism in Kenya
1. Kenya is a class society

Today Kenya is sharply divided into classes, of various
categories of the rich few and the majority poor; the capitalists
and the workers; the exploiters and the exploited; the oppressors
and the oppressed. Few families own land the size of districts
while the majority of Kenyans are either landless or eke out a
living in small pieces of land. Most of the Kenyan people live in
abject poverty but a few are billionaires who are among the
richest people in the world. While the country continues to
receive foreign aid, there is enough wealth concentrated in the
hands of the privileged few which is capable of bringing about
development to the country. Class divisions are obvious in
residential locations, living standards and lifestyles, particularly
in urban areas. The education system does not offer equal
opportunities to children and youth but instead it is structured to
increase the gap between the rich and the poor, the rural and
urban areas. The rich access the best social services, clean and

is hardly accessible to the poor who form the majority of the
citizens. The economy is geared towards maintaining the status
quo of capitalism and imperialism. The rich are far better placed
to exercise all citizens and human rights denied to the poor.

social justice but also national unity. Under circumstances of
such glaring inequalities, it is not easy to remove the bitterness,
